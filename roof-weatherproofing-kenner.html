Kenner roof weatherproofing at a glance

Weatherproofing is four named upgrades that make a roof hold up better in a wind event. All four together earn a FORTIFIED certificate, and the discount law that follows names no parish limit. What matters here is the order, because one of the four keeps working after the covering has blown off.

The four upgrades

Named by IBHS

Sealed roof deck, ring-shank fastening on the covering, reinforced edges at the drip edge and starter course, and a wind-rated covering (ASTM D7158 Class H or D3161 Class F). Together they meet the IBHS FORTIFIED Roof standard. Impact resistance is a separate optional Hail Supplement, not part of the base standard.

The intake route in Kenner

Email or drop-off

Weatherproofing is a set of upgrades usually done during a reroof, and Act 239 of 2025, recodified by Act 881 of 2026 as R.S. 37:3737(I), makes a reroof a permitted and inspected job. Metairie, Gretna, Harahan and Baton Rouge all take that application through the MyGovernmentOnline portal; Kenner does not. Kenner applications go to kennerpermits@kenner.la.us or in person at the Inspection and Code Enforcement Department, 1926 18th Street, on (504) 468-4064. Timing matters here because the Evaluator has to be booked before the work starts.

Grant available

By lottery, when open

Louisiana Fortify Homes covers a large share of the cost of a full FORTIFIED reroof in an eligible parish. Jefferson Parish is one of the eligible parishes, so Kenner homeowners are eligible when a round is open. The award must be secured before work starts; the state does not reimburse a FORTIFIED reroof funded on the homeowner's own initiative. The grant runs as a lottery and rounds open and close, so the storm damage page carries the current amount and eligibility, and the LDI page carries the current status.

Discount law

La. R.S. 22:1483, no parish limit

Louisiana R.S. 22:1483 requires insurers who write property policies in the state to provide an actuarially justified adjustment (in substance, a discount, credit, rate differential or other) on structures built or retrofitted to a fortified standard. The statute names no parish limit, so a Kenner homeowner with a certificate is on the same footing as a St. Tammany one.

The certificate itself

5 years, IBHS Evaluator

A FORTIFIED certificate is issued by an IBHS-trained Evaluator, not by a roofer, and it runs for five years before it has to be renewed. A roofer builds the roof to the standard. The Evaluator inspects and issues the certificate against it.

The timing trap

Retrofit now, certify later cannot

The three conditions on the discount are Evaluator, certificate before adjustment, and a policy providing wind coverage. All three matter, and one of them is a timing trap: work done before an Evaluator is engaged does not become certifiable in arrears. Book the Evaluator first, build to spec, then present the certificate.

Weatherproofing is a bundle of four named upgrades. Each one does a different thing, each one costs a different amount, and the difference between what they buy is bigger than most quotes make it look. Doing all four earns a FORTIFIED certificate, which triggers a state insurance discount law that names no parish limit at all. Doing one or two of them earns nothing on paper. What it earns in practice depends heavily on which ones.

The most important thing to know before pricing a weatherproofing job is that the four upgrades are not equal. Three of them protect the covering. One of them protects the house even when the covering is gone. The IBHS framing calls the sealed roof deck a "second line of defence" for exactly that reason: an ordinary underlayment is not sealed at the deck seams and wind-driven rain moves through it, so a covering that blows off in a storm ends the roof. A sealed roof deck stops the rain at the deck itself, so a covering that blows off does not become a flooded house. That is the upgrade to sequence first if the budget cannot cover all four.

The grant and the discount law, the three conditions on the money

Two separate pieces of money can be earned by weatherproofing to the FORTIFIED standard: a state grant for the upgrade work, and an ongoing insurance discount required by state law. Both come with conditions, and the timing on one of them is the difference between paying and not paying.

The grant, by name

Louisiana Fortify Homes

A state grant administered by the Louisiana Department of Insurance that funds a large share of the cost of a full FORTIFIED reroof in an eligible parish. Jefferson Parish is one of the eligible parishes, so Kenner homeowners are eligible when the round is open. Like the discount, the grant is not paid in arrears: the award has to be in hand before the work starts, and the state will not reimburse a FORTIFIED reroof that was funded on the homeowner's own initiative. The dollar amount and the current status live on the LDI Fortify Homes page, and on the storm damage page here.

The certificate

5 years, IBHS Evaluator

A FORTIFIED certificate is issued by an IBHS-trained Evaluator against the standard the roof was built to. It is not issued by a roofer and it is not a warranty. It runs for five years before renewal, and renewal needs another Evaluator visit. Book the Evaluator before the work, not after.

The discount law

R.S. 22:1483

Louisiana R.S. 22:1483 requires insurers who write property policies in the state to provide an actuarially justified adjustment (in substance, a discount, credit, rate differential, deductible adjustment or other) on structures built or retrofitted to the fortified standards. The statute names no parish limit, so a Kenner certificate is on the same footing as a St. Tammany one.

The three conditions

On the discount

The R.S. 22:1483 discount runs on three conditions: the certificate is issued by an IBHS-trained Evaluator, it is presented to the insurer BEFORE any adjustment takes effect, and the policy actually provides wind coverage. All three matter, and the second is a timing trap because retrofit work done before an Evaluator is engaged does not become certifiable in arrears.

What the discount is worth

The size of the adjustment sits between the insurer and the Louisiana insurance commissioner, so this page does not predict a percentage. What R.S. 22:1483 requires is that some adjustment exists, actuarially justified, whenever a fortified retrofit is documented and presented. The size varies by policy and by insurer, and it is worth asking a homeowner's own carrier what its published adjustment is for FORTIFIED certificates before pricing the retrofit.

This page states what the statute requires; it does not state or predict what any individual policy will pay. Louisiana R.S. 37:2159.1 stops a roofing contractor from interpreting an insurance policy on a homeowner's behalf, and that boundary is respected here.

The sequence that works, and the one that does not

The sequence that works is book the Evaluator, agree the scope to the FORTIFIED standard, execute the work with the Evaluator inspecting at the required stages, take the certificate, and present it to the insurer before the next renewal. That is the order R.S. 22:1483 is written around.

The sequence that does not work is retrofit the roof, then decide to seek certification, then look for an Evaluator to sign off in arrears. An Evaluator cannot inspect what has been closed up, so the retrofit becomes uncertifiable and the discount route is closed. Book the Evaluator first.

Why the deck comes first on a Louisiana roof

Three things about this climate push the value of weatherproofing toward the deck and the edges rather than the covering itself. Wind arrives from the Gulf, rain arrives in volume, and the failure that costs a house is not the covering going, it is the water coming through what is left.

Wind removes the covering more often than it removes the deck

A named storm can strip a covering off a house without touching the deck underneath, and a deck under a lost covering is what determines whether the house is flooded or dry the next morning. Ordinary underlayment is stapled and not sealed, so wind-driven rain passes through it. A sealed roof deck stops the rain at the deck seams, so a covering loss becomes a temporary uncovered deck rather than a house-wide interior claim. That is the whole IBHS "second line of defence" framing.

Wind-driven rain is the failure mode, not the wind alone

A category one storm can push rain sideways through gaps that a level rainfall never reaches. Ring-shank fasteners keep the covering on for longer, reinforced edges keep wind from prying under the covering at its edges, and a wind-rated covering resists uplift for longer than a stock one. None of those matters if the covering is gone. The sealed deck matters especially because it is the one upgrade that is still working when the covering is not.

East bank, coastal exposure

Kenner is on the east bank of the Mississippi and, at 66,448 people, is the sixth largest city in Louisiana. The wind field of a coastal storm reaches both banks and the difference is not enough to lean a weatherproofing decision on. The four upgrades are worth doing on either bank, and the sequence is the same on both. A permitted reroof in Kenner goes in by email to the Inspection and Code Enforcement Department rather than through a portal, which is worth knowing before the timeline is set.

What does roof weatherproofing actually mean

A set of upgrades that make a roof hold up better in a wind event. The IBHS FORTIFIED Roof standard names four of them: sealed roof deck, ring-shank fastening on the covering, reinforced edges, and a wind-rated covering meeting ASTM D7158 Class H or D3161 Class F. All four together earn a FORTIFIED Roof certificate. Class 4 impact resistance is a separate optional Hail Supplement, not part of the base standard.

Why does the sealed roof deck come first

Because it is the only one of the four upgrades that keeps working after the covering has failed. Ordinary underlayment is stapled and not sealed at the deck seams, so wind-driven rain moves through it once the covering is gone. A sealed roof deck stops the rain at the deck, so a covering loss becomes a temporary uncovered deck rather than a flooded house. IBHS calls it a second line of defence for that reason.

What if I cannot afford all four upgrades

Prioritise the sealed roof deck. Then reinforced edges and ring-shank fastening at any reroof, both of which are near marginal in cost during a reroof. The wind-rated covering is often already met by stock architectural shingles, so it is usually the least expensive of the four rather than the most. Note that partial upgrades do not earn a FORTIFIED Roof certificate or the associated discount. Class 4 impact resistance is a separate decision.
