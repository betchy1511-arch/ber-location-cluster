Metairie elastomeric coatings at a glance

An elastomeric coating is a liquid applied film that stretches and recovers as the roof moves through a day of Gulf heat. The acrylic type is defined by ASTM D6083, which sets minimum cured film properties. It runs roughly $1.75 to $4 per square foot and is specified on dry film thickness.

The standard

ASTM D6083

The standard specification for liquid applied acrylic elastomeric roof coating. A product either meets it or it does not, and the datasheet will say.

Minimum elongation

100 percent

The standard sets a minimum elongation at break for the cured film, which is what lets it move with the roof rather than split across it.

Minimum tensile

200 psi

The other half of the same requirement. Elongation without tensile strength is a film that stretches and tears.

Estimated cost per sqft

$1.75 to $4

Installed, on a Metairie commercial roof, and it moves with the specified thickness and the amount of prep.

Reinforced at details

Polyester fabric

Where it is wrong

Standing water

What the standard covers and where elastomeric is the wrong choice

ASTM D6083 tells you the cured film will stretch, hold together and survive weathering. It does not tell you the roof is a candidate, that the insulation is dry, or that acrylic is right for your drainage. Those are survey questions, and on a roof that ponds the honest answer is a different chemistry.

ASTM D6083

What it actually sets

The standard specification for liquid applied acrylic elastomeric roof coating. It sets minimum cured film properties including elongation at break, tensile strength, permeance, water swelling, adhesion, tear resistance, fungi resistance and low temperature flexibility after accelerated weathering. A datasheet either claims compliance or it does not.

The limit of the standard

It is a film test

ASTM D6083 describes how the cured film behaves in a laboratory. It says nothing about whether your roof is a candidate, how well the coating will adhere to your particular substrate, or whether the drainage suits an acrylic at all. Compliance is a floor for the product, not a verdict on the installation.

The cure window

Real scheduling constraint

A water dispersed coating needs a dry period to cure to the properties the standard describes. Metairie humidity and an afternoon storm pattern narrow that window, so the roof is staged in sections around the forecast. A film applied into weather it cannot cure in does not reach its specified properties.

Jefferson Parish permit

Confirm the scope

Jefferson Parish publishes no commercial roofing permit exemption. Its no permit list covers a change of roof covering only, and gutter work, on one and two family dwellings, and a separate clause allows minor ordinary repairs without a permit only with the approval of the Building Permits Department, case by case. Big Easy Roofing confirms the scope with the parish before starting.

Reinforcement is where coatings actually fail

A coating almost never fails in the open field. It fails at the seams, the curbs, the drains and the parapet junction, because those are where the roof moves most and where the film is thinnest over an edge.

A proper elastomeric specification embeds polyester reinforcing fabric into the coating at every one of those details. It is a small share of the material cost and most of the difference in how long the system lasts, which is why it is a named line in the scope.

Comparing two elastomeric bids

Bids on this work are frequently not comparable, because the cheaper one is a thinner film of a product that does not claim the standard, over less preparation, with no reinforcement.

Put the six specification lines side by side and the gap usually explains itself. Where the roof turns out not to be a coating candidate at all, a Metairie commercial coating survey will say so before any chemistry is chosen.

Why elastomeric suits a Metairie roof

Three things about this climate make a stretchable film worth specifying. Daily thermal movement that works every seam on the roof, a UV load that ages a surface faster than the test data assumes, and buildings whose rooftop equipment keeps the membrane in constant local motion around the curbs.

Daily thermal movement

A dark commercial membrane in Metairie can swing a long way between a July afternoon and the following dawn, and it expands and contracts every one of those cycles. The stress concentrates at seams, curbs and parapet junctions.

A film with a minimum 100 percent elongation at break moves with that instead of splitting across it. This is the whole argument for elastomeric over a rigid coating, and it is why the standard sets an elongation minimum at all.

Flexibility after weathering, not just when new

A coating that is stretchable out of the pail and brittle after four Gulf summers has failed at the only point that mattered. This is why ASTM D6083 measures elongation at break after accelerated weathering as well as initially.

It is the single most useful number to ask a supplier for, and the one a datasheet is most likely to quote only in its initial form. Ask for both figures.

Movement around rooftop equipment

Curbs under mechanical units are the busiest joints on a commercial roof. They move thermally like everything else, and they also take vibration from the equipment and traffic from whoever services it.

That combination is why reinforcement at curbs is not optional in the specification, and why a coating that was rolled straight over a curb flashing without fabric tends to open at exactly that line first.

What does elastomeric actually mean

That the cured film stretches and recovers rather than cracking when the roof moves. For the acrylic type the word has an objective definition: ASTM D6083, the standard specification for liquid applied acrylic elastomeric roof coating, which sets minimum cured film properties including elongation at break and tensile strength. A product either claims compliance with it or it does not.

What does ASTM D6083 require

Minimum properties for the cured film, including an elongation at break of at least 100 percent and a tensile strength of at least 200 psi, along with requirements for permeance, water swelling, adhesion, tear resistance, fungi resistance and low temperature flexibility after accelerated weathering. It is the reason to ask a bidder which standard their product meets rather than accepting the word elastomeric on its own.

How much do elastomeric coatings cost in Metairie

Estimated ranges. Acrylic elastomeric runs roughly $1.75 to $4 per square foot installed on a Metairie commercial roof, and the figure moves with the specified dry film thickness and how much preparation the existing surface needs. Those are market estimates rather than a quote; the written specification on your building carries the real number.

Elastomeric or silicone for my roof

It comes down to drainage. Acrylic based elastomeric films soften under water that sits, so on a roof that holds water, silicone is usually the better specification. On a roof that drains properly, acrylic elastomeric is the cheaper system and takes a recoat readily. The drainage survey decides it, and the chemistry comparison in full is on the commercial coating page.

What is the difference between two coats and ten dry mils

One describes effort, the other specifies a product. Dry film thickness is what the warranty is written against, and on an elastomeric system it is also what determines whether the cured film actually reaches the elongation and tensile properties ASTM D6083 sets, since those are measured on a film of a given thickness. A thin application of a compliant product is not a compliant system.

Why does reinforcement matter

Because coatings fail at details rather than in the open field. Seams, curbs, drains and parapet junctions are where the roof moves most and where a liquid film is thinnest over an edge. Embedding polyester reinforcing fabric into the coating at those points is a small part of the material cost and most of the difference in service life.

Can you coat over a roof with wet insulation

No, and candidacy is settled before the chemistry is even discussed. Whether a roof can take a coating at all is covered on the commercial coating page; what matters here is that no amount of elongation in the cured film compensates for water underneath it. An elastomeric system is specified only once the survey has cleared the assembly.

How long will an elastomeric system last

Manufacturers write warranty terms in years against a specified dry film thickness, the preparation and often an approved applicator, and terms commonly run in tiers. What a system delivers in practice depends on the thickness actually applied, the reinforcement at the details and the condition of the roof underneath, which is why all three are named in the scope.

Can an elastomeric roof be recoated

Yes, and on an acrylic system the reason is worth knowing. ASTM D6083 measures elongation after accelerated weathering as well as when new, because the property that matters is whether the film is still flexible years in. A system that has held its flexibility takes a compatible recoat readily; one that has gone brittle is a different job.

Will a coating stop my roof leaking

Not by stretching over it. Elongation lets the cured film follow the roof as it moves, which is what stops new cracks forming at the details, but an existing breach is repaired and reinforced during preparation before any field coat goes on. A film bridged over an open defect fails at exactly that line.
