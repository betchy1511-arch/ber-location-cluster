When a coating is the right answer and when it is money wasted

A coating is worth doing on a roof that is weathering but still sound, and worth refusing on one that is not. Four checks settle it: how much of the insulation is wet, whether the seams still hold, whether the roof drains, and whether the existing surface will accept a coating at all.

Wet insulation

Rules it out

This is the first check and it is usually the decisive one. Insulation that has taken on water does not dry out under a coating any more than it does under a membrane, and sealing it in means the underlying failure carries on unseen. Isolated wet areas are cut out and replaced before coating; widespread moisture means the roof needs recovering or replacing instead.

Ponding water

Decides the chemistry

Plenty of older Metairie flat roofs hold water somewhere. Silicone tolerates standing water better than the other chemistries and is the usual answer where ponding cannot be designed out. Where the ponding is structural, adding drainage or tapered insulation is part of the scope rather than something a coating compensates for.

Jefferson Parish permit

Confirm the scope

Jefferson Parish publishes no commercial roofing permit exemption. Its no permit list covers a change of roof covering only, and gutter work, on one and two family dwellings, and a separate clause allows minor ordinary repairs without a permit only with the approval of the Building Permits Department, case by case. Big Easy Roofing confirms the scope with the parish before starting.

The manufacturer warranty

Written on thickness

Coating warranties are written against a specified dry film thickness, the prep and often an approved applicator, and they are quoted in years against those conditions. That is why the scope names the mils rather than the number of coats, and why a cheaper quote at a thinner spec is not the same product.

Mils, not coats, and the arithmetic behind it

A coating quote that says two coats tells you nothing. What matters is the dry film thickness, which comes from the wet rate and the volume solids: a coating at 50 percent volume solids laid at 20 wet mils dries to about 10 dry mils.

Two quotes can both say two coats and differ by half the material. Big Easy Roofing states the specified dry film thickness and the gallons per square that produce it, so the comparison between bids is a real one.

Where a coating genuinely pays

On a sound membrane, a coating costs a fraction of a re-roof, is far less disruptive, sends nothing to landfill and can usually be recoated at the end of its term rather than torn off. On the right roof it is very good value.

On the wrong roof it is poor value, because it costs real money and buys little. Where the survey says the membrane is at the end of its life, a Metairie commercial re-roof is the honest answer and we will say so.

What Metairie roofs do to a coating

Three local conditions shape every Metairie coating specification. Roofs that hold water long after the rain has stopped, a UV and heat load that ages the surface faster than the manufacturer's test data assumes, and a humidity pattern that narrows the window in which a water based coating can cure properly.

Water that sits

Ponding is common on the older Metairie flat roofs we survey, and it is the single condition that most coatings handle badly. Acrylics soften under standing water. Silicone is formulated to live with it, which is why it is specified so often here.

Where water stands more than about 48 hours after rain, the specification either goes to silicone or the scope includes correcting the drainage. Coating over a pond and hoping is not a specification.

Heat and UV load

A reflective coating drops the surface temperature of a dark membrane substantially, which slows the ageing of everything under it and takes load off the air conditioning below. That is a large part of why coating pays on a Gulf Coast building.

It also means reflectivity retention matters. Silicone holds dirt and loses reflectivity as it weathers, acrylic holds its brightness better but does not like standing water, and that trade off is the real decision on most Metairie roofs.

The cure window

Water based coatings need a dry window to cure, and Metairie humidity plus an afternoon storm pattern narrows it. A coating applied into weather it cannot cure in will not reach the properties the warranty is written against.

That is a scheduling problem rather than a product problem, and it is why the crew watches the forecast and stages the roof in sections rather than opening the whole area at once.

How much does commercial roof coating cost in Metairie

Estimated ranges for 2026. Acrylic runs roughly $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot installed, acrylic elastomeric $1.75 to $4, polyurethane $2.50 to $5 and silicone $2 to $6, with published figures varying widely by source and by specified thickness. Those are market estimates. The written specification on your building, after the survey, carries the real figure.

Is my roof a candidate for coating

It depends on four things and the first is decisive. How much of the insulation under the membrane is wet, whether the seams still hold, whether the roof drains, and whether the existing surface will accept a coating. A membrane that is weathering but sound is an excellent candidate. One with widespread wet insulation is not, and coating it seals the water in.

Can you coat over wet insulation

No, and anyone offering to should be asked what happens to the water. Wet insulation does not dry out under a coating, it has already lost its thermal value, and the failure arrives on schedule regardless of what is on top. Isolated wet areas are cut out and replaced as part of the prep. Widespread moisture means the roof needs recovering or replacing.

Silicone or acrylic for a Metairie roof

Often silicone, because a lot of older Metairie flat roofs hold water somewhere and silicone tolerates standing water better than the alternatives. Acrylic is cheaper, more reflective and easier to recoat, and it is the better choice on a roof that drains properly. The decision is made on the drainage rather than on the price per square foot.

What does dry film thickness mean and why does it matter

It is the thickness of the coating once it has cured, and it is what the warranty is written against. It comes from the wet application rate and the coating's volume solids: a product at 50 percent volume solids laid at 20 wet mils dries to about 10 dry mils. Two quotes can both say two coats and deliver very different thicknesses, which is why the specification states mils and gallons per square.

How long does a commercial coating take

Days rather than weeks on most Metairie roofs, typically one to five working days depending on area, how much prep the surface needs, access and the weather, and it is an estimate confirmed at survey. Many buildings are able to trade through the work, which is agreed as part of the programme rather than assumed. Water based coatings need a dry window to cure, so the work is staged in sections around the forecast rather than opening the whole roof at once.

How long does a roof coating last

Manufacturers write warranty terms in years against a specified dry film thickness, the prep and often an approved applicator, and terms commonly run in ten, fifteen and twenty year tiers. What you get in practice depends on the thickness actually applied and on the roof underneath, which is the argument for specifying mils rather than coats.

Can a coated roof be recoated later

Yes, and that is a large part of the appeal. At the end of a coating term a compatible recoat generally restores the system without a tear off, provided the substrate has been maintained and the original system is still sound. That makes the whole life cost look very different from a membrane that has to be removed and landfilled.

Will a coating fix my leak

Only if the leak is coming through a weathered surface. If it is coming in at a seam, a curb, a drain or a penetration, those are repaired as part of the prep before any coating goes on, because a coating over an unrepaired defect is a coating over a leak. Where the source is not obvious, a leak detection survey comes first.

Do I need a Jefferson Parish permit to coat a commercial roof

Do not assume you do not. The parish publishes a roofing exemption for one and two family dwellings only, with no commercial equivalent, and its minor repairs clause allows ordinary repairs without a permit only with the approval of the Building Permits Department, decided case by case. Big Easy Roofing confirms the scope with the parish before starting rather than guessing.
