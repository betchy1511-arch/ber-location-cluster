Does it need a permit

Not for panel work

Replacing soffit panel is not the re roofing that the 2025 rule makes permitted and inspected. Where there is structural work behind the panel, that position changes and we confirm it with the correct office first.

What holds the run up

F channel plus fascia nailer

A soffit is caught at the wall by a receiver, typically F channel or J channel, and at the front by a nailer fixed to the fascia. The panel is not doing structural work, so the two edges are the whole fixing story.

The vinyl fastening rule

Nail centred and loose

Vinyl panel is slotted for a reason. The fastener goes in the middle of the slot and is left a fraction proud, roughly the thickness of a dime, so the panel can slide as it expands and contracts. Nailed tight or nailed to one end of the slot, it buckles.

Typical fastener spacing

12 to 16 inches

Manufacturers commonly specify 12 to 16 inches on centre through the receiver and nailer, tightened where a run is exposed to wind uplift. Wider spacing leaves the eave for the wind to work at, which is where a coastal storm undoes a panel.

Materials fitted

Vinyl, aluminium, fiber cement

Vented and solid panels in all three. Vinyl is the value option, aluminium holds a long run straight and takes a heavier paint film, and fiber cement is the closest match to a painted timber soffit.

Soffit is the underside of the eave, and on most houses it is doing two jobs at once. It closes the eave against wildlife and weather, and where it is vented it is the intake half of the attic ventilation. The panel choice covers the first job by material and the second job by perforation. What covers the third question, service life, is the fixing.

That last point is worth stating plainly, because it is where soffit money gets wasted. A run fitted in the same panel and at the same price will last three times as long on one house as on another, and the difference is almost always at the fasteners. Vinyl needs room to move, aluminium needs the joints allowed to open and close, and fiber cement needs pre-drilling and a fastener that will not corrode in the eave. Get the fixing right and the panel decision is between three good options. Get the fixing wrong and the panel choice barely matters, because none of them survive the wrong installation for long.

How much intake the attic actually needs is a separate calculation and it is set out on the roof ventilation page, which is the sibling that carries the net free area figure. This page is about what makes the panel stay where it was fitted.

How each panel is actually fixed

The panel choice sets the fixing rules, not the other way round. Vinyl and aluminium move enough with temperature that the fixing has to plan for it, and fiber cement moves very little but is heavy and cracks if the fastener is wrong. These are the working rules by material.

The permit position, and why the code cares about intake

Panel work on an eave is not a reroof, so it sits outside the state rule that makes re roofing permitted and inspected. What is worth knowing is that since 2025 a permitted reroof on a house is judged against the code chapter that ventilation lives in, which is why the eave is not simply a cosmetic detail.

Who issues the permit

For an address inside the Harahan city limits, the City of Harahan Regulatory Department on (504) 737-6765. For an address in the unincorporated part of Jefferson Parish, the parish permits office. Panel replacement

Not a reroof

Replacing soffit panel is not a replacement of the roof covering, so it sits outside the permit and inspection requirement Act 239 of 2025 introduced. The current codification is R.S. 37:3737(I) after Act 881 of 2026 recodified. Where the work turns structural behind the panel, we put the position to the correct office rather than deciding it ourselves.

Why intake sits inside the code now

IRC chapters 8 and 9

Since 1 August 2025 a permitted reroof on a one or two family dwelling is judged against IRC chapters 8 and 9 with Louisiana amendments, and chapter 8 is the roof and ceiling chapter that attic ventilation requirements sit in. So a panel that seals a vented eave is not just a cosmetic problem on the next reroof.

The screen behind the vent

Keeps the eave closed

A vented panel is an opening, so the insect screen is what stops the intake becoming an entrance. On this coast the eave sees wasps, birds and squirrels working at any gap, and the screen is the difference between a ventilated eave and an occupied one.

Why the fixing spec belongs on the quote

Because the panel is not the part that fails first. Two quotes at the same price per foot and the same panel can deliver very different service lives, and the reason for that is almost always the fixing detail. Nothing on either quote makes it visible unless somebody writes it down.

Ask for the receiver named at the wall, the fastener spacing, and the expansion allowance on the material. On a vinyl quote the centred slotted fastener is the one sentence that separates a run that will still be flat in five years from one that will not. It costs a bidder nothing to state and it turns two quotes that read alike into two quotes you can compare.

Intake and exhaust have to match

A soffit is half of a system. Exhaust at the ridge without intake at the eave does not simply underperform, it can pull its make up air from the house below through gaps in the ceiling, which moves conditioned air into the roof space.

So a new soffit is worth doing at the same time as any ventilation work rather than separately, and it is worth checking the intake is not blocked from the inside by insulation pushed into the eave. Baffles are what stop that, and they are covered on the roof ventilation page along with the net free area figures.

What an eave here is actually up against

Three local conditions decide how well a soffit stays fixed here, and none of them is about the panel itself. It is a hot climate, a wet climate, and one where wind arrives at the eave first. All three are dealt with at the fixing rather than at the material.

Temperature swings work the fixing

A dark eave in a Louisiana summer sees surface temperatures well above the air temperature and drops away sharply after a storm rolls through. Vinyl expands and contracts more than aluminium at the same temperature swing, and both move more than fiber cement. That movement has to go somewhere, and if the fastener has held the panel still it goes into a buckle or a split. Which is why the centred slot rule on vinyl and the expansion allowance on aluminium are not optional details.

The eave stays damp between storms

Sustained humidity means an eave here rarely dries out completely, and NOAA Atlas 14 records a 100 year 60 minute rainfall rate of 5.39 inches per hour for the Harahan coordinates as a measure of how hard the rain can arrive. That is severity rather than a sizing basis. What it means for the eave is that fasteners spend a lot of their life in damp timber, so the corrosion class of the fastener matters as much as the spacing. Stainless or hot dipped galvanised on fiber cement, and coated on aluminium.

Wind gets at the eave first

The eave is where wind gets under the roof, so a soffit is the first thing a coastal gust will try to lift. That is why fastener spacing is a wind concern rather than a weight concern, and why an exposed elevation gets the spacing tightened at the ends and at the corners. A run held at 24 inches on centre gives the wind twice as much of a lever as one held at 12.

What does soffit actually do

Two things at once. It closes the underside of the eave against weather, birds, wasps and squirrels, and where it is vented it is the intake half of the attic ventilation. The second job is invisible from the ground, which is why it is the one that gets specified badly.

Why is fastener spacing on a soffit worth caring about

Because the eave is where wind gets under the roof, and a panel held at wide spacing gives the wind a longer lever to work with. Standard practice is 12 to 16 inches on centre and tightened at ends and corners, and an exposed elevation on this coast is worth the tighter end of that range rather than the wider end.

What is the centred slot rule on vinyl

Vinyl panel is manufactured with slotted holes because it expands and contracts noticeably with temperature. The fastener goes in the centre of the slot, driven only until the head is touching, then backed off a fraction. Roughly the thickness of a dime is the number a lot of installers work to. If the panel cannot slide, it buckles.

What about the fixings on fiber cement soffit

Fiber cement gets pre-drilled and screwed with a corrosion resistant fastener, typically stainless or hot dipped galvanised. Nailing fiber cement instead of screwing it, or fastening without pre-drilling, is the reason a fiber cement panel cracks at the fixing point later. It is heavy enough that fastener choice is not a nice to have.

Vinyl, aluminium or fiber cement

Vinyl is the value option and copes well with damp. Aluminium holds a long run straight and takes a heavier paint film. Fiber cement is the closest match to a painted timber soffit, which matters on an older house where the look of the eave is part of the elevation. On a coastal eave any of the three works if it is fastened correctly.

What is the receiver at the wall for

It catches the wall side edge of the panel and gives the run somewhere to slide into as it expands. F channel is the common one, J channel is used where the wall detail asks for it. A run without a proper receiver either leaks water back against the sheathing or has to be trimmed and sealed to the wall, which stops it draining.

What is net free area and where do I read about it

Net free area is the open area a vented panel gives you per linear foot of eave, and it is what decides how much air the attic can draw in through that panel. Because the range across published products is very wide, that figure comes off the datasheet for the named panel rather than off the material. How much your attic needs in total is on the roof ventilation page.

Is a solid soffit panel wrong

Not at all, in the right place. Solid panel is correct on a gable end and anywhere the eave is not meant to be feeding the attic. It is only wrong when it is run along an eave that the ventilation is relying on, which happens more often than it should because solid stock is on the van and vented stock is a separate order.

Does the Harahan climate change how a soffit is fitted

Yes, at the fastening rather than the panel. Long hot summers with sharp cooldowns after storms work the panel harder than a milder climate would, and the eave sees coastal wind first when a storm comes through. That is why fastener spacing gets tightened at exposed ends and corners here rather than run flat.
