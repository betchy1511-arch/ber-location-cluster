LaPlace hail damage at a glance

Hail damage on a LaPlace roof is often invisible from the street and rarely leaks straight away. It bruises shingles and knocks granules loose, which shortens the life of the covering. Hail rarely announces itself

Bruises, not holes

A hailstone can crack the mat under a shingle without breaking the surface. The roof looks intact from the ground and may not leak for a long time, while the damaged area loses protection faster than the rest.

What hail does to a roof in LaPlace

LaPlace is hot and humid for most of the year, which is hard on an asphalt covering that has already been bruised. Hail damage does not stay the same size. Once the protective surface is broken, the damaged area ages faster than the rest of the roof around it.

A bruise ages faster than the roof around it

Granules protect the asphalt from sunlight. Where hail knocks them loose, the exposed patch dries and cracks sooner, so a roof with a few years left can lose some of them in the damaged area.

That is why hail is treated as a question of remaining service life rather than as a leak repair, and why "it is not leaking" is not the same as "it is fine".

Looking from the ground is not an assessment

Bruised shingles look like undamaged shingles from the street. The assessment is done on the roof, slope by slope, with the soft metals checked as a guide to where the strikes landed.

If you want the whole roof put on record rather than only the hail question answered, a roof inspection does that.

A confined area is usually a repair

Where the damage is limited to one area and the rest of the covering is sound, a roof repair is usually the cheaper and correct answer.

Where it is spread across several slopes, repeat repairs rarely make sense and a replacement, permitted through the parish, is the honest recommendation.

How do I know if my roof has hail damage

Often you cannot tell from the ground. Dents in vents, flashing and gutters show the roof was hit. Bruised shingles and granule loss are found on the roof itself, and the pattern across the slopes is what separates hail damage from ordinary ageing.

Does hail damage always cause a leak

No. Hail often cracks the mat under a shingle without breaking the surface, so the roof may not leak for a long time. The damaged area still ages faster than the rest, which shortens the life of the covering.

Are granules in my gutters a sign of hail

They can be, but granule loss is also normal on an ageing roof. On its own it proves nothing. It is read alongside dents in soft metals and bruising on the shingles.

What does the parish permit form ask of my roofer

The St. John the Baptist Parish Residential Permit Application asks for the contractor's business, name and licence number, and a note on the form says current registration or licence, when applicable, is required at the time of submittal. Check the licence with the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.

A roofer asked me to sign something after the storm. What is it

Read it before you sign. The parish permit form says all owners must be listed and must sign the application, and where someone else applies, it asks whether there is a letter of authorization or a signed contract. A paper handed over at the door may be one of those.

What happens if work starts before the permit

The parish residential permit application states that all fees will be doubled for after-the-fact permits, and when information provided on an application is falsified. A reroof needs the permit under La. R.S. 37:3737(I), so ask the Permit Division on (985) 651-5565 before any work starts.
