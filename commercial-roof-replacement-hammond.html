Hammond commercial roof replacement at a glance

A commercial reroof here runs on two decisions and two documents. Whether the existing assembly is stripped or recovered, and which office issues the permit, because Hammond is one of the few places in this region where a commercial roofing permit scale is actually published rather than quoted on request.

The City publishes a commercial scale

$50, then $5 per thousand

The City of Hammond permit application prices commercial renovation, repair and roofing at $50.00 up to $10,000 of construction value. Above that the form shows an initial $50 and a rate of $5 per thousand, without saying whether the rate runs on the whole value or only the part above $10,000, so the calculation is confirmed with the City. Commercial is priced higher than the residential line on the same form.

The Parish line does not split the two

Renovations, either way

The Tangipahoa Parish schedule prices Renovations on fair market value, listing $20 for a value up to $10,000 and $2.00 per thousand at $10,000 or more. The Renovations line itself is not labelled residential or commercial, so which category a commercial reroof is filed under is confirmed with the office.

There is an annual registration as well

$100, expiring 31 December

The Parish charges a contractor and subcontractor registration of $100, valid from the date of issue to 31 December of that calendar year. Because the published expiry is 31 December rather than twelve months from issue, a registration taken out later in the year has less than a full year left on it.

The code reference is different from a house

IBC Chapter 15

Louisiana requires permitting and inspection of reroofing for buildings other than one and two family dwellings in compliance with International Building Code Chapter 15, at La. R.S. 37:3737(I)(1). The Parish states it implemented the 2021 building codes and the 2020 NEC effective 1 January 2023.

Two records come out of the job

Permit file and warranty file

The permit and its inspections are one record, held by the authority. The membrane manufacturer's warranty is a separate record with its own conditions. Enhanced system warranties commonly require an approved installer and a manufacturer review, while basic material warranties often do not, so requirements vary by manufacturer and warranty type. Neither record substitutes for the other.

Starting before the permit issues costs more

City of Hammond

The City application states that failing to get the permit before work begins results in a stop work order and a doubling of the City permit fees, and that the City may require removal of the work begun and impose monetary fines.

The permit route here has a published price

Most places in this region make a facilities manager ring for a commercial roofing permit figure. Hammond prints one. What still has to be settled is which office covers the building, because the two scales differ and one of them also carries an annual registration.

Permitting and inspection are compulsory

Statewide since 2025

The State Licensing Board for Contractors states that Act 239 requires permitting and inspection of roof construction and reroofing of residential and commercial structures for any project beginning on or after 1 August 2025. It is carried at La. R.S. 37:3737(I).

Inside the city limits

City of Hammond

The Building Department at 219 E Robert St, open Monday to Friday 8am to 4pm, on (985) 277-5655. Its published commercial renovation, repair and roofing scale starts at $50.00 up to $10,000 of construction value.

Outside the city limits

Tangipahoa Parish

The Parish Permit Office at 15485 West Club Deluxe Road, on (985) 542-2117. It adopted the 2021 building codes and the 2020 NEC with effect from 1 January 2023, and also handles the contractor registration.

Ask for the classification on a commercial job too

Verify with the Board

Commercial roofing carries its own licence classification, separate from the residential roofing rule that applies to houses. Ask any contractor bidding this work for their Louisiana state licence number and its expiry date, and verify the number and the classification with the State Licensing Board rather than taking it from a letterhead.

Failing to obtain inspections is a violation

Act 422 of 2025

The Board states that Act 422 makes it a violation of the law for a contractor to fail to obtain a permit, to begin work before the permit is issued, to misrepresent the value or scope of work, or to fail to obtain inspections as required by law. The memo sets it out.

The registration date that catches people out

The Parish contractor and subcontractor registration is $100 and runs from the date of issue to 31 December of that calendar year. It is not a rolling twelve months, so a registration taken out in the autumn expires within weeks of a job that runs into the new year.

On a phased commercial project that crosses the year end, the registration renewal is a programme item rather than an afterthought. It sits alongside the re-inspection and certificate of occupancy charges the same schedule publishes.

Why the value declared on the application matters

Both published scales are calculated on the value of the work, so the figure entered on the application drives the fee. Act 422 separately makes it a violation for a contractor to misrepresent the value or scope of work, which puts an accurate figure on both sides of the same requirement.

That is a reason to have the scope written and priced before the application is filed rather than after, and it is the same discipline that keeps the permit file and the manufacturer warranty file agreeing with each other later.

Replacing a roof over a building still in use

Most commercial reroofs here happen over an occupied building, which changes the sequence more than it changes the specification. Three constraints do the work, and all three are programme decisions taken before the first fastener rather than problems solved on site.

The building keeps operating, so the roof comes off in sections

Work is broken into areas that can be stripped, dried in and made watertight within the same day, rather than opening more deck than the weather allows. That is slower than a vacant building and it is the only way to protect what is underneath.

It also decides where the tear off starts. Areas over plant, stock or anything that cannot be moved are sequenced deliberately, not left until last by default.

Rooftop plant and penetrations are the real programme

Units, ducts, conduit and drains all have to be lifted, isolated or worked around, and each one is a detail that will leak if it is reinstated badly. On most commercial roofs the penetrations take longer than the field.

Where equipment has to be shut down or lifted, that is scheduled with the building rather than assumed, because a crane slot and a tenant notice are not things that can be arranged on the morning.

Tear off or recover is decided on the deck, not the budget

A recover leaves the existing assembly in place and is quicker and cheaper, but it is only honest where the deck and the existing insulation are dry and sound, and where the number of existing layers still permits it.

Wet insulation left under a new membrane fails the new roof and voids most manufacturer warranties. Where a survey shows moisture, the tear off is the answer even though it is the more expensive one.

Should the existing roof be torn off or recovered

It is decided on the deck. A recover is quicker and cheaper and is only honest where the deck and existing insulation are dry and sound and the number of existing layers still allows it. Wet insulation under a new membrane fails the new roof and voids most manufacturer warranties.

Can the work happen while the building stays open

Usually. The roof comes off in sections small enough to be stripped, dried in and made watertight the same day, and areas over plant or stock are sequenced deliberately. It is slower than a vacant building, which is a programme consideration rather than a specification one.

The rooftop penetrations and the weather windows, rather than the field of the roof. Units, ducts and drains take longer than open area, and on an occupied building the amount of deck that can be opened at once is limited by what can be closed again the same day.
