The damage that costs most is the damage you cannot see

Wind rarely removes a whole roof. What it does is break the seal strip that bonds each shingle course to the one below, and a course that has lost its seal lies back down looking completely normal. The roof passes a look from the driveway and then leaks in the next ordinary rain.

That is why an assessment after a storm is done on the roof rather than from the ground, and why a stain that appears indoors weeks after the weather is so often traced back to it. If a storm went through and nothing looks wrong, that is worth a proper look rather than a relief.

What a written storm scope should contain

Each damage type named separately, the repair described, a price against it, and a photograph of the thing being claimed. If the scope is one line and one number, there is nothing in it that you or anyone else can check.

It should also say plainly where repair stops and replacement starts. A covering damaged in several planes on an older roof is usually a replacement, and a scope that quietly prices it as a very large repair is not doing you any favours either.

And it should be dated, because when the damage was recorded is part of the record.

What Gulf storms do to a roof here

Three mechanisms account for the damage this climate produces, and they need different responses. Two of the three are invisible from the ground, which is the single most useful thing to know before deciding for yourself whether your roof came through it intact.

Uplift at the edges and the ridge before the field

Wind does not press evenly on a roof. It accelerates over the ridge and curls at the eaves and rakes, so uplift is highest exactly where the covering is least supported. That is why damage starts at edges and ridges and works inward rather than beginning in the middle of a slope.

It also means the first courses to fail are the ones a ladder can reach, which is the one piece of luck in it. An edge assessment tells you a great deal about whether the rest of the roof took the same loading.

Wind driven rain going where gravity would not take it

Rain arriving sideways gets under laps, behind flashings and through vents that shed falling water perfectly well. A roof can be entirely sound against vertical rain and still let water in during a storm, which is why a leak that only appears in high wind is a different diagnosis from one that appears in any rain.

That distinction matters, because the fix is usually a detail rather than the covering. Replacing shingles on a roof that leaks only sideways will not stop it.

Hail, which is judged by count rather than by appearance

Hail damage is not decided by how bad the roof looks. It is assessed by counting impacts within a measured test square on each slope, because that is what separates a covering that has lost service life from one that is merely marked.

It is also the damage type most often called wrong in both directions. A roof can look peppered and test clean, or look fine and be bruised through. The count is the answer, and it is done on the roof.

The estimated range runs from about $500 for a few wind lifted courses to $20,000 where the covering is damaged across several slopes and the job becomes a replacement. It is priced by damage category rather than as one storm total, so each line can be checked.

Will you handle my insurance claim

No, and be wary of a roofer who says they will. Louisiana law restricts what a roofing contractor may tell you about your own cover. What we do is photograph and categorise the damage and price the repair in writing, which is what your insurer needs from a roofer. The coverage question is between you and them.

My roof looks fine after the storm, is it

Possibly not. A very common form of storm damage is a broken seal strip, where wind lifts a shingle course, breaks the bond and lets it lie back down looking normal. It shows up as a stain indoors after the next ordinary rain rather than as anything visible from the ground.

How is hail damage decided

By counting impacts within a measured test square on each slope, not by how the roof looks. A roof can look peppered and test clean, or look fine and be bruised through the covering. The count is done on the roof and photographed.

Do I need a permit for a storm repair

Not for a localised repair. The 2025 rule covers roof construction and re roofing, so where a storm takes the roof into a full re roof it is permitted and inspected by East Baton Rouge, and we confirm the position with Permits and Inspections before that work starts.

When does a storm repair become a replacement

Broadly when the covering is damaged in more than one plane on a roof that already has age on it, because each separate repair is then buying a short extension on a system near the end of its life. We put both numbers in writing so the decision is yours.
