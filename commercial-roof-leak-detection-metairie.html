Metairie commercial leak detection at a glance

A commercial leak survey answers two questions that a walked inspection cannot. Where the water is actually entering the assembly, and how much of the insulation under the membrane is already wet. The second decides whether you are buying a repair, a recover or a replacement, so it is worth knowing first.

What it establishes

Entry and extent

The breach itself, and the boundary of the wet insulation around and downstream of it, marked on a roof plan.

Methods used

Four

Infrared thermography, capacitance meter, electronic leak detection and, on the right roof, a controlled flood test.

Permit authority

None

A survey is a diagnostic visit and not construction, so Jefferson Parish requires no permit for it.

What you receive

A mapped report

Wet areas outlined on a plan with the suspected entry points marked, photographed, and a scope priced against the map.

Why it matters

It sets the strategy

Isolated wet areas get cut out within a repair. Widespread moisture rules out a recover and a coating, which changes the budget entirely.

Best conditions

Clear evening

Infrared works on the heat the roof gives back after sunset, so the scan is run after a sunny day rather than at any convenient hour.

Water entering a flat roof travels. It gets in at a seam, a fastener, a curb or a drain, runs across the deck or sideways inside the insulation, and appears inside the building at the first joint or low point it reaches, which can be a long way from the breach. Chasing the stain is how a roof gets repaired three times in a year without the leak stopping.

The survey works the other way round. It finds the breach, then maps how far the water has already spread inside the assembly, because those are different facts and the second one is the expensive one. A roof with one breach and two wet bays is a repair. The same breach with a third of the insulation saturated is a re-roof conversation, and the only difference between the two invoices is whether anyone measured.

Method descriptions are general to commercial roofing practice. Which methods suit a particular roof depends on the membrane, the insulation type, the deck and the access, and that is established at the survey rather than promised in advance.

Four survey methods and which roof each one suits

No single method works on every commercial roof. Each reads a different physical property, each has conditions it needs, and each has roofs it cannot read at all. The survey usually combines two, and knowing why the second one was used is a fair question to ask any contractor who runs one.

What the survey decides and why it is worth doing first

A moisture survey is inexpensive relative to the decisions it informs. Where the insulation turns out to be widely wet, that finding alone rules out a coating and a recover, and it is the input most often missing when a roof is quoted from the surface. It is one part of the decision rather than all of it.

Extent, not just location

The expensive fact

Finding the breach is the easy half. The half that moves the budget is how far the water has already travelled inside the insulation, because that is what rules a recover or a coating in or out. Isolated wet bays get cut out within a repair. Widespread saturation means the assembly has to come off.

Infrared is an evening job

Physics, not preference

Wet material holds the day's heat longer than the dry material beside it, so the scan reads that difference as the roof gives its heat back after sunset. That means a sunny day, a clear dry evening and a dry surface. A scan run at the wrong time produces a clean image on a wet roof.

Jefferson Parish permit

None for a survey

A moisture survey is a diagnostic visit rather than construction, so no permit is required for the visit itself. Permits become relevant only if the survey leads to work, and a commercial replacement is a plan scoped permit under the 2021 International Building Code.

Confirmed on foot

Every single anomaly

Thermal and capacitance scans both produce anomalies that turn out to be something else: a patch of ballast, a buried curb, a previous repair, a warm duct below the deck. Every anomaly is walked and checked before it is drawn on the map, because an unverified scan is a picture rather than a survey.

Why the third repair is the expensive one

A building that has been repaired three times in a year for the same leak is usually not suffering from bad repairs. It is suffering from a breach nobody located and insulation nobody measured, so each repair is aimed at the stain rather than the source.

A survey is what breaks that pattern, because it looks for the source and the extent rather than the symptom. Where it finds a defect rather than saturation, the fix is scoped as a Metairie commercial roof repair priced against the same map.

The map is the deliverable

What you keep is a roof plan with the wet areas outlined, the suspected entry points marked, and photographs tied to locations. That is a document you can put in front of a contractor, a consultant or a building owner and get comparable answers from.

It is also the baseline for the next survey. Two maps a year apart show whether an area is spreading, which is the difference between a roof that is stable and one that is quietly failing.

Why Metairie flat roofs hide their leaks

Three local conditions make leaks harder to find here than the textbook suggests. Rain arrives faster than older drainage can clear it, the roofs are crowded with mechanical equipment that hides its own damage, and warm wet insulation stays warm and wet rather than drying out between storms.

Water that outruns the drains

A Gulf downpour can put more water on a roof in an hour than an older Metairie building was drained for. Water backs up, finds the weakest joint, and gets in at a point that is dry and unremarkable the rest of the year.

That is why a survey looks at how the roof behaves in rain as well as how it looks in sun. A leak that only appears in heavy rain is usually a drainage problem presenting as a membrane problem.

Equipment hiding the damage

The membrane around rooftop units takes the most traffic and the most abuse, and it is also the hardest area to inspect, because the damage is frequently under or behind the unit where nobody can see or scan it cleanly.

Curbs also throw off thermal scans, since a warm duct below the deck reads much like wet insulation from above. Both are reasons every anomaly near equipment gets confirmed on foot rather than trusted from the image.

Insulation that never dries

In a drier climate a small amount of trapped moisture can dry back out between storms. Here it generally does not: the humidity is high, the assembly stays warm, and the water has nowhere to go.

So a small wet area found early stays small only if it is dealt with. Left alone through a Gulf summer it spreads through the boards, and the cost of the eventual fix rises with the square footage of saturation rather than with the size of the original breach.

We have been repaired three times and it still leaks

That is the usual reason people call for a survey, and it is rarely a sequence of bad repairs. It is normally one breach that was never located plus wet insulation nobody measured, so each repair was aimed at the symptom. A survey finds the entry point and maps how far the water has already travelled, which is the fact that decides what the real fix is.

What methods do you use

Up to four, chosen from the roof rather than in advance. Infrared thermography reads the heat wet material holds after sunset. A capacitance meter reads the change in dielectric constant that moisture causes below the membrane. Electronic leak detection finds the electrical path a breach opens between a wetted surface and the deck. A controlled flood test suits some sound decks. Usually two are combined.

Why does the infrared scan happen in the evening

Because of what it measures. Wet material holds the day's heat longer than the dry material beside it, and the scan reads that difference as the roof gives its heat back after sunset. It needs a sunny day, a clear dry evening and a dry surface. A scan run in the wrong conditions can produce a clean looking image on a roof that is soaking.

Are there roofs infrared cannot read

Yes, several. Ballasted roofs, roofs that are wet on the surface, heavily reflective coatings that flatten the thermal signal, and overcast conditions that stop the roof shedding heat cleanly. That is precisely why a survey is not a single instrument, and why every anomaly is confirmed on foot before it is drawn on a plan.

What do I actually get at the end

A roof plan with the wet areas outlined and the suspected entry points marked, photographs tied to those locations, and a scope priced against the map. It is a document you can hand to a contractor, a consultant or an owner and get comparable answers from, and it is the baseline for comparing against the next survey.

How does the survey change what I spend

Mostly by ruling options out. Isolated wet areas can be cut out and replaced within a repair. Widespread saturation rules out a coating and a recover, because both would seal the water in, which usually leaves a full replacement. Moisture is not the only factor in that decision, but it is the one most often unmeasured.

Do you have to open the roof to check

Not usually. The scan methods are non destructive. A small test cut is sometimes taken to confirm what a scan is reading and to see the layer build up, and it is patched immediately. On a roof heading for a re-roof decision that cut is worth taking, because the layer count also decides whether a recover is even permitted.

Do I need a permit for a leak survey

No. A survey is a diagnostic visit rather than construction, so Jefferson Parish requires no permit for the visit itself. Permits become relevant only if the survey leads to work, and a commercial replacement is a plan scoped permit under the 2021 International Building Code with architect or engineer stamped plans.

Should we survey before buying a building

Yes, and before the price is settled. A walked inspection tells you what the roof looks like; a moisture survey tells you how much of the assembly is already wet, which is the difference between inheriting a roof with years left and inheriting a replacement. On a large flat roof that is one of the biggest single numbers in the deal.
