Hail is different from every other kind of roof damage because the thing it breaks is not the thing you can see. A hailstone that strikes an asphalt shingle hard enough can fracture the mat inside it while leaving the surface looking close to normal, and it can knock the granules off a patch that then weathers far faster than the shingle around it. Neither shows from the ground, and neither necessarily leaks for a long time.
That is why hail is assessed by counting rather than by looking. A square is marked out on each slope, the impacts inside it are counted, and the count is recorded against that slope. A roof with a scattering of marks across one face is a very different proposition from a roof carrying a consistent count on every face, and the difference between them is a number rather than an opinion. Anyone can recount it afterwards, which is the point of doing it that way.

Which makes the order of events matter
Photograph, then permit, then strip
Once a roof is stripped, nobody can go back and photograph what was on it. Since 1 August 2025 that is not only a documentation problem for the homeowner, it is a gap in the file the parish inspection is built from.
Hail damage is not an emergency, and that is the trap
A hail damaged roof frequently does not leak. The mat is fractured and the granules are gone from a patch, and the shingle above still sheds water for a season or several. Nothing drips, nothing stains, and the natural conclusion is that the roof came through it.
What has actually changed is the rate at which that roof ages. Exposed asphalt weathers far faster than a granular surface, and a fractured mat has lost the strength it was relying on. So the damage is real and the consequence is deferred, which is the worst combination for getting it looked at.
Being careful about who you let on the roof
A hailstorm brings people to the door, and some of them were not in Baton Rouge the week before. Check the contractor is licensed by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors, get the price in writing with the date on it, and do not sign anything that assigns your claim or your benefits to the contractor.
Be wary in particular of anyone who wants to get on the roof before anything is photographed. Foot traffic marks a roof, and a roof that has been walked before it was recorded is a roof whose evidence is now harder to read.

Hail in Baton Rouge arrives
in a different season from the wind
The two things that damage roofs here do not turn up at the same time of year, which is why a house can take two unrelated hits in one twelve month period and why an annual inspection is worth more here than a post storm one alone.
The hail season is spring
National Weather Service Storm Prediction Center records put Louisiana hail firmly in spring, with March, April and May carrying the great majority of recorded events. It arrives with the squall lines that form when Gulf moisture meets a strong front, which is a completely different mechanism from a tropical system.
The practical consequence is that the roof inspection people book after hurricane season is looking for the wrong damage six months late. Hail that fell in April has had a summer to weather before anybody goes up to look at it.
Which is why the two get confused
Wind and hail break a roof in different ways and are documented differently. Wind breaks the seal strip that bonds each shingle course to the one below, and the course lies back down looking untouched. Hail fractures the mat and strips granules in defined patches.
A roof can carry both, and where it does, each is recorded separately rather than rolled into one storm total, because they are different defects with different repairs. The storm damage page covers the wind side of that in full.
Older Baton Rouge housing stock, older coverings
A roof at the end of its service life and a roof damaged by hail can look similar from the ground and are not the same thing, and telling them apart honestly matters more than either finding on its own.
Where a covering is simply old, we will say so rather than describe age as impact damage. Where hail has genuinely broken an older covering, the age of the roof does not make the damage less real. Both of those go in the written assessment, and neither is a comment on what any insurer will do with it.

How can I tell if my roof has hail damage
From the ground, generally you cannot, and that is the honest answer. The reliable early signs are not on the roof at all: dents in gutters, downspouts, vent caps and flashings, because soft metals record an impact that a shingle can absorb without showing much. If the soft metals are dented, the roof is worth counting properly.
What is bruising and why does it matter
A bruise is a spot where the hailstone has fractured the mat inside the shingle while leaving the surface looking close to normal. It is felt as a give under a thumb rather than seen. It matters because the shingle has lost the strength it was relying on while still appearing sound, so it is damage that a visual check misses.
My roof has hail damage but it is not leaking, do I need to do anything
A hail damaged roof frequently does not leak at first, and that is what makes it easy to ignore. What has changed is the rate at which the roof ages, because exposed asphalt weathers far faster than a granular surface and a fractured mat has lost strength. Getting it documented while the evidence is fresh costs nothing and is worth doing.
What is a test square
A marked out ten foot by ten foot area on a single slope. The impacts inside it are counted and the count is recorded against that slope, then the same is done on each other slope. It turns the assessment into a number that anyone can recount rather than an impression that nobody can check.
Will you handle my insurance claim
No, and be wary of a roofer who says they will. Louisiana law restricts what a roofing contractor may tell you about your own cover. What we do is photograph the roof, count the impacts per slope and price the work in writing, and hand that to you to use with your insurer.
Should I sign anything a roofer brings to the door after a hailstorm
Not on the doorstep, and not anything that assigns your claim or your benefits to the contractor. Get the price in writing with the date on it, check the contractor is licensed by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors, and take the time to read it.
Does hail damage work need a permit in East Baton Rouge
A repair does not, because a repair is not the reroofing that La. R.S. 40:1730.23(K) names. A full replacement does, and it is inspected, so the permit and the photograph set are part of the job from the start rather than an afterthought.
Why do you photograph the old roof before stripping it
Two reasons that happen to point the same way. It is the only record of what the damage looked like, and East Baton Rouge asks for 16 geotagged photographs on a reroof of which two are of the existing roof material. Once the roof is stripped neither of those can be recovered.
