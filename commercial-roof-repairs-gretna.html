Gretna commercial roof repairs at a glance

A commercial roof in Gretna is normally low slope, which changes what fails and which code governs it. Since 2025 a commercial re roof also needs its own permit and inspection under state law, and which office issues it depends on which side of the city limit the building sits.

Low slope and metal

Single ply TPO and EPDM, modified bitumen, built up gravel systems, and standing seam or R panel metal. Repairs cover seams and laps, penetrations and curbs, drains and scuppers, parapet and coping details, and storm damage. Which code applies

IBC Chapter 15

Louisiana R.S. 37:3737(I)(1) sends buildings other than one and two family dwellings to International Building Code Chapter 15, as adopted by the state. Only (I)(2), the houses, goes to the residential code. Louisiana is on the 2021 editions with state amendments.

One office, two desks

The City of Gretna Building Department and the Historic District Commission sit at the same address, 740 2nd Street, so a downtown property that needs both a building permit and a Certificate of Appropriateness is handled by two desks in one building rather than two offices in two towns.

The survey

Assembly, drainage and moisture

Warranty

Workmanship on the repair

The repair carries our workmanship warranty. Where the membrane is still inside a manufacturer warranty we work to the manufacturer detail rather than against it, because an incompatible patch can void the remaining cover on the whole roof. The terms are the manufacturer's, so we confirm what they require before starting.

A commercial roof rarely announces itself the way a house roof does. There is often no attic to stain and no ceiling directly under the deck, so the first sign is a damp patch in an office two bays away from the actual defect, or an insulation board that has been quietly wet for a year. By the time it is visible inside, the assembly below the membrane has generally been holding water for some time.

That is why a commercial repair starts with the drainage rather than the leak. Low slope roofs are designed to move water to a small number of drains or scuppers, and the first thing to check on a repeat repair is whether water is getting there at all: a drain proud of the deck, a scupper blocked since the last storm, or a section that settled and now holds a pond after every rain. Fix the defect and leave the pond, and that seam is the one likeliest to go again.

The second thing that has changed is administrative rather than physical. Under the 2025 state law a commercial reroof now needs its own roofing permit and inspection. In Gretna that means one of two routes, city or parish, and which of them applies is settled by the city boundary rather than by the postal address. On top of that, a downtown Gretna building that sits inside the local historic district can trigger a Certificate of Appropriateness where a change to the roof is visible from public right of way, so the visibility question is asked before the scope is priced.

Which office and which code cover a commercial roof here

The 2025 Louisiana roofing law is usually described as a rule about houses. It is not. The statute has two limbs and a commercial building is on the other, which changes the code and the paperwork. Which office you file with depends on the city limit, and a downtown property can bring the Historic District Commission in.

What actually changed

Permit and inspect

La. R.S. 37:3737(I) requires every municipality and parish that issues construction permits to permit and inspect roof construction and re roofing. That covers commercial and residential structures alike. The requirement came in through Act 239 of 2025 and the framework was moved to its current chapter by Act 881 of 2026.

Your building is on the commercial limb

IBC Chapter 15

Subsection (I)(1) sends every building other than a one and two family dwelling to International Building Code Chapter 15, as adopted by the Louisiana State Uniform Construction Code Council. Only (I)(2), the houses, goes to the residential code. Louisiana moved to the 2021 editions with state amendments on 1 January 2023.

Inside the city limits

City of Gretna

The City of Gretna Building Department at 740 2nd Street handles permits inside the limits, on 504.363.1563. The general commercial fee schedule is set in the municipal code and confirmed at the counter for a specific scope of work.

Outside the city limits

Jefferson Parish

The parish handles unincorporated area permits, and a commercial reroof runs through the general Jefferson Parish commercial permit route rather than a roofing line. The parish publishes no commercial roofing rate we could verify, so pricing is confirmed at the parish counter when the application is submitted.

A downtown property can bring the Historic District Commission in

Gretna's Historic District Commission reviews any exterior change visible from public right of way on a property inside the local historic district. On a low slope commercial roof that mostly does not apply, because a flat roof is rarely visible from the street. Where it can apply is a storefront with a visible mansard, a facade-facing coping change, or rooftop equipment that shows above the parapet from the corridor.

The rule is one line on the City Building Department page and it reads broadly, so a downtown property that sits inside the local district is worth confirming with the office before the scope is fixed. The Historic District Commission and the Building Department are in the same building at 740 2nd Street, which keeps the sequence short: the Certificate of Appropriateness is settled before the building permit is issued, and both live at one address rather than two.

A repair that is entirely on the field of the roof and not visible from public right of way normally sits outside the visibility test, but that is a question for the City rather than a call we can make on your behalf. If any part of the scope changes what a passer by can see, raise it at the start.

The 25 percent rule is a Florida rule and it is not what triggers an upgrade here

It comes up on commercial work constantly, usually from an owner who has read that repairing more than a quarter of the roof forces a full replacement. That idea comes from Section 706 of the Florida Building Code, Existing Building, which carries its own conditions and applies in Florida. Louisiana is on the International codes and has no equivalent percentage trigger.

What decides it on a commercial roof here is the scope of work and the permit itself. Once the office issuing the permit classes the job as a re roof it is permitted and inspected on the Chapter 15 route, whatever fraction of the area that happens to be. The useful question is not what percentage you are replacing, it is whether the office is going to class the work as a re roof, and that is answerable before you start.

There is a real percentage rule the state does carry, and it catches people out because it sounds like the Florida one. Under federal flood requirements adopted locally, work counts as substantial improvement once its cost reaches 50 percent of the market value of the structure, for buildings inside a Special Flood Hazard Area. That is a federal flood rule rather than a roofing rule, it measures the value of the work against the value of the building rather than the area of the roof, and it only applies in the flood zone. On the west bank a lot of the Gretna commercial area falls inside a flood zone, so if the building sits in a Special Flood Hazard Area settle where the work stands against that threshold with the office that permits the building before the scope is fixed rather than after.

What fails first on a west bank commercial roof

Three patterns come up on low slope roofs in a Gulf coast climate on the west bank of the Mississippi. Each one is a reason a previous repair did not last, and each is fixable, but only if the thing causing it is fixed at the same time rather than patched over.

Standing water on a roof that never had the fall it needed

A low slope roof is not a flat roof, and the difference is drainage. Where a deck has settled, an insulation layer has compressed, or a drain sits slightly proud of the surface, water stops moving and stays. In sustained Gulf heat that pond then cooks the membrane above it, and a seam that might otherwise have had years left in it can go early.

The tell is a dark ring on the membrane that is still visible when the roof is dry. A repair inside that ring without correcting the fall is a repair with a known expiry date, so we price the drainage correction beside the patch and let you choose with both numbers in front of you.

Wind uplift on a west bank building without the intervening bulk

A Gretna commercial building sits closer to open water than most of the metro, with less intervening bulk between it and a storm track that comes off the Gulf. On a low slope roof that is not primarily a covering question, it is a peel edge question: parapet flashings, coping details and the perimeter fields of the membrane are where wind uplift finds a start.

That is where a repeat storm repair is worth having done once rather than twice. A coping cap re fastened on a proper pattern, a peel edge repaired back to the deck rather than surface patched, and the perimeter membrane fasteners re set into sound decking are the details that hold the next storm. The interior field of the roof is usually the least urgent thing on the survey after a wind event.

Rooftop equipment growing on a roof that was not scoped for it

A lot of west bank commercial buildings have taken more rooftop plant over their life than the original scope allowed for: HVAC units added, condensers replaced with heavier ones, kitchen exhaust cut in later. Each of those is a penetration or a curb the original roofer did not detail, and each is a potential leak path.

The tell is a repeat drip that only shows up in certain wind directions or after a specific run of hot days. On the survey we walk every curb and penetration rather than looking only at where the stain reports to, because a leak that repeats after a repair is usually somewhere the previous fix did not visit.

How much does a commercial roof repair cost in Gretna

It depends on the assembly and on what is under the membrane, so we survey before quoting rather than pricing over the phone. On the permit side, the City of Gretna processes commercial work through its general commercial permit route, with the permit fee set in the municipal code and confirmed at the counter. The parish route is priced from its general commercial schedule rather than a roofing line. Both are permit side costs, not the cost of the roof itself.

Does a commercial re roof need a permit here

Yes. La. R.S. 37:3737(I), introduced by Act 239 of 2025 and renumbered by Act 881 of 2026, requires parishes and municipalities that issue construction permits to permit and inspect roof construction and re roofing, and Gretna and Jefferson Parish both do. Which office you file with depends on whether the building sits inside the Gretna city limits.

My building has a Gretna address, so which office issues the permit

Not necessarily the City of Gretna. The Gretna postal area extends beyond the city boundary, and the unincorporated part is permitted by Jefferson Parish. It is worth settling the boundary question before an application is filed, because the two offices run different intake for a commercial job.

My building is in downtown Gretna, does the Historic District Commission apply

Possibly, and it is a question for the City rather than one we can answer for a specific address. The Commission reviews exterior changes visible from public right of way on properties inside the local historic district. On a low slope commercial roof that mostly does not apply, because the flat field is not visible from the street, but a visible mansard, coping change or new rooftop equipment showing above the parapet can bring the visibility test in. Both the Commission and the Building Department are at 740 2nd Street, so it is a short sequence when it does apply.

Which building code applies to a commercial roof in Louisiana

International Building Code Chapter 15. La. R.S. 37:3737(I) splits it explicitly: subsection (I)(1) sends buildings other than one and two family dwellings to IBC Chapter 15, and only (I)(2) sends houses to the residential code. Louisiana is on the 2021 editions with state amendments.

Does the 25 percent rule apply to a commercial roof here

No. It is Section 706 of the Florida Building Code, Existing Building, and Louisiana is on the International codes with no equivalent percentage trigger. On a commercial roof what matters is whether the office issuing the permit classes the work as a re roof, because that is what puts it on the Chapter 15 permit and inspection route.

What is the difference between commercial and industrial roofing

Mostly scale, access and what the roof is carrying. Commercial usually means a low slope membrane over an occupied building: shops, offices, churches, apartment blocks. Industrial tends to mean larger areas, more rooftop plant, and sometimes chemical or heat exposure that rules out particular membranes. The permit route is the same for both, because the statute divides on whether it is a one and two family dwelling, not on the trade the building is used for.

What happens if you find wet insulation below the membrane

We stop, itemise the affected area on an amended scope, and get it signed before carrying on. Patching a membrane over saturated insulation is a patch with a known expiry date, so it is called out rather than covered up. Where the wet area is small and localised it is dealt with as part of the repair; where it is widespread the honest conversation is about a re roof rather than a repair.

Can you repair the roof while we stay open

Usually yes. Commercial repairs are normally worked around your trading hours, with the noisy and disruptive stages flagged in advance so you can plan around them. Where work has to happen outside normal hours we say so and price it separately rather than adding it to your invoice later.
