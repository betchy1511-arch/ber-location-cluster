The hours straight after a storm are the ones where the most damage gets done and the most people get hurt, and almost none of that is about roofing skill. It is about sequence. A Metairie property that has just taken a named storm can have a live conductor lying in the side yard, a gas riser sheared by a limb, standing water over an unseen kerb, and a roof deck that will not hold a person, all at once, and none of those are visible from a phone call.

So the first thing a crew does on arrival is not climb. It is walk the perimeter, judge what is live, what is unstable and what is holding water, and decide whether anyone should be on the roof or in the building at all. On a house where the ceiling is bowed and heavy with water, the honest answer is sometimes that nobody goes inside until it is dropped deliberately and safely.

Only after that does roofing work start, and even then it is temporary by design. The aim in the first visit is to stop water entering, remove what is dangerous and record the property exactly as we found it. Repair is a separate job done in daylight with a written scope, and treating the two as one is how people end up paying emergency rates for work that was never urgent.

Safety, the law and what we will not do in the dark

Emergency work carries hazards ordinary roofing does not, and some of the rules that apply are not roofing rules at all. Utilities, structural safety and what a contractor may say about your insurance all bear on the first visit, and each is handled the same way every time.

Downed conductors

Assume energised

Entergy says to assume every downed line is energised, and that a line can kill even if it does not spark, hum or move. Stay away from anything touching it, a tree, a fence or a vehicle, and watch for lines hard to see in puddles. If you must move away, shuffle with your feet together rather than taking large steps, because voltage spreads out through the ground. Report it to Entergy on 1-800-968-8243. Our crew stays out of the area and works nowhere near it until the utility has attended.

Structure before access

No blind climbing

Storm damaged or displaced decking, impact damaged framing and a ceiling loaded with water can all have badly reduced load capacity, and some of that cannot be judged reliably from ground level. The deck and framing are evaluated before a ladder goes up, and where they will not safely carry a crew we say so rather than risk it.

Generators after the storm

Never backfeed

Entergy is explicit that you must never power house wiring by plugging a generator into a wall outlet. Backfeeding can electrocute utility crews and the neighbours on your transformer, and it bypasses the built in circuit protection. Keep the generator dry and out of the rain, plug appliances directly into it, and if it has to feed the house wiring have a qualified electrician install the proper equipment first.

What we record and what we will not say

Documentation only

We photograph the property as found and write a good faith itemised estimate. Louisiana law prohibits a contractor from interpreting policy coverage or duties, so what your policy does is a question for your insurer or a licensed public adjuster, not for us.

The water inside is a different trade, and we will say so

A roofer stops water coming in and documents what came in. Drying a saturated structure, running dehumidification, lifting floors and dealing with what grows afterwards is water restoration work, and it is a different trade with different equipment.

Being clear about that boundary matters, because a wet building that is closed up and left develops problems that cost more than the roof did. We tell you plainly what we have stopped, what is still wet, and what needs a restoration contractor rather than implying the roof work covers it.

What to do before we arrive, and what not to

Useful and safe: get everyone out from under any bowed or dripping ceiling, and move what you can from under the leak. If the panel is wet or damaged, or you would have to cross water to get to it, leave the building and call an electrician or the emergency services instead. Never touch a panel while standing in water.

Not safe, and worth saying plainly: do not get on the roof, and do not go into the attic if the ceiling below is loaded with water. On generators, Entergy is explicit that you must never power the house wiring by plugging a generator into a wall outlet, which is called backfeeding and can electrocute utility crews and neighbours on the same transformer. Keep it dry and out of the rain, plug appliances straight into it or into a properly rated outdoor cord, and if it has to connect to the house wiring have a qualified electrician fit the right equipment first. Debris on the ground can be left for us, it is not worth an injury in the dark.

Why the queue works the way it does across Metairie after a storm

After a widespread event several hundred Metairie properties call in the same night, and no contractor can reach them all at once. Understanding how the order is set is the difference between waiting anxiously and knowing what to do meanwhile.

Risk sets the order, not call time

Occupied properties with an active hazard come first: exposed conductors, a compromised structure, or water reaching electrics. Then occupied properties taking water. Then unoccupied and secondary damage, then everything else.

That means a call at two in the morning about a dripping ceiling in an empty rental may be reached after one at six about a bowed ceiling over a bedroom. It is not first come first served, and being told that honestly is more useful than being given an arrival time nobody can keep.

We do not send crews up during the event

Wind is the reason. Working at height while a named storm is still passing is how people are killed, so the line stays open, the address is logged and advice is given, but nobody is dispatched onto a roof until wind speed drops to a safe working range.

The same applies to lightning within striking distance and to standing water around a property where the ground underneath cannot be seen. Waiting is not reluctance, it is the only responsible answer.

The first visit is deliberately temporary

Emergency weatherproofing is sized to the opening rather than to the roof, and it is not meant to be a repair. Trying to do permanent work in poor light, in wind, on a deck that has not been properly surveyed produces work that has to be redone.

So the visit ends with the property safe, dry and photographed, and the real repair is measured and written up afterwards. Where the roof turns out to need replacing rather than repairing, that is priced on the Metairie emergency roofing page and in the repair scope.
