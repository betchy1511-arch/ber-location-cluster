Permits, the code and what a recover is not allowed to hide

A Metairie commercial re-roof is a permitted project. Jefferson Parish applies the 2021 International Building Code to commercial work and its published commercial submittal requirements call for architect or engineer stamped plans, with no roofing exemption published. The code also sets hard limits on when a recover is allowed at all, and those are not negotiable at the quoting stage.

When a recover is not allowed

Three hard stops

The reroofing provisions prohibit a roof recover where the existing roof or covering is water soaked or has deteriorated so far that it is no longer an adequate base for additional roofing, where the existing covering is wood shake, slate, clay, cement or asbestos cement tile, and where the roof already has two or more applications of any type of covering. Any one of those means tear off.

Jefferson Parish permit

Plans required

Commercial re-roofing takes a Jefferson Parish commercial building permit rather than the flat residential re-roof form, with the 2021 International Building Code applied. Jefferson Parish's published commercial submittal requirements call for a complete set of plans by a Louisiana registered architect or civil engineer, with a statement of supervision on the first sheet, and the parish publishes no roofing specific exemption from that. It also notes that circumstances particular to an application may dictate deviations from its standard requirements, so the scope is confirmed with the Building Permits Department before anything is filed. Big Easy Roofing runs that process as part of the project.

The state licence threshold

$50,000

The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors requires a commercial licence on commercial projects with a value of $50,000 or more, including labour and materials, which nearly every commercial re-roof reaches. Big Easy Roofing holds a board licence. Ask any bidder for theirs on a project this size, because here it is a condition of the work rather than a formality.

Uplift and the perimeter

Where storms start

A re-roof is the opportunity to correct the edge. Perimeter and corner zones take the highest uplift pressures in a Gulf storm and are where failures begin, so the fastening pattern is denser there by design. Getting the edge metal, the termination and the corner fastening right is worth more in a hurricane than an upgrade in the field of the roof.

The test cut answers what nobody can see

How many layers are on the roof, what they are, and whether the insulation is wet cannot be established from the surface, and all three change what is legal and what it costs. A small test cut and a moisture survey settle it before anyone commits to a route.

Skipping that step is how a recover gets sold on a building that needed a tear off. The extra layer goes on, the water stays underneath, and the failure arrives well inside the warranty period with nobody willing to honour it.

Where a recover genuinely wins

On a dry, single layer roof with a sound deck, a recover is faster, materially cheaper, keeps the business trading with less disruption and sends far less to landfill. It is often the right answer and we will say so.

It is not a way of avoiding a replacement that is already due. Where a survey finds widespread moisture, a recover costs the same as it would on a sound roof and buys a fraction of the life, which is the worst outcome available on this decision.

What decides a Metairie re-roof in practice

Three things settle most Metairie commercial re-roofs before anyone opens a catalogue. How much water is trapped in the existing assembly, how many layers are already up there, and whether the deck and the perimeter can carry the new system through a Gulf storm season.

Trapped water is the whole decision

Insulation that has taken on water does not dry out under a membrane. It has already stopped delivering its R value, it adds weight the deck was not designed to carry indefinitely, and it will corrode fasteners and steel deck from underneath.

A moisture survey maps it before the route is chosen. Isolated wet areas are cut out and replaced as part of a recover. Widespread moisture means tear off, and finding that out first is the single most valuable thing on the project.

Counting the layers properly

Building records are often wrong about how many roofs are up there, especially on Metairie buildings that changed hands or were re-roofed after a storm without a permit being pulled. The code allows a maximum of two coverings, so a building already carrying two has to be torn off.

A test cut settles it in minutes and is worth doing even when the paperwork looks clear, because being wrong about this is the difference between a compliant project and one an inspector stops.

The edge is what a hurricane finds

Uplift pressure concentrates at the perimeter and hardest at the corners, and that is where commercial membrane failures start in a Gulf storm. Once the edge lifts, the wind gets under the field and takes the rest.

A re-roof is the natural moment to rebuild the edge metal, the terminations and the corner fastening to current requirements rather than matching whatever was there. It is a small share of the project cost and a large share of the storm performance.

What is the difference between a recover and a re-roof

A recover installs a new membrane over the existing roof covering without removing it. A tear off strips everything back to the deck and starts again. Both are re-roofing. A recover is cheaper, quicker and less disruptive, but the building code only allows it under specific conditions, so the first job is establishing whether it is available on your building rather than deciding whether you want it.

When does the code not allow a recover

Three hard stops. Where the existing roof or covering is water soaked or has deteriorated to the point that it is no longer an adequate base for additional roofing. Where the existing covering is wood shake, slate, clay, cement or asbestos cement tile. And where the roof already carries two or more applications of any type of covering. Any one of those means a tear off.

How do I find out how many roof layers my building has

A test cut, which takes minutes and is patched immediately. It is worth doing even when the building records look clear, because records are frequently wrong on this, particularly on buildings that changed hands or were re-roofed after a storm without a permit. Being wrong about the layer count is the difference between a compliant project and one an inspector stops.

How much does commercial re-roofing cost in Metairie

Estimated ranges. A recover typically runs $4 to $12 per square foot depending on the system and how much insulation or cover board goes into the build up. A full tear off and replacement runs $5 to $20 per square foot on the system selected. Those are market estimates. The written scope on your building, after the survey, carries the real figure.

How long does a commercial re-roof take

Two to six weeks on a typical Metairie commercial building, depending on the system, the size of the roof and how much of the existing assembly has to come off. A recover on a dry single layer roof sits at the shorter end. A full tear off with deck repair sits at the longer end, and weather moves both.

Do I need a Jefferson Parish permit

Yes. Commercial re-roofing requires a Jefferson Parish commercial building permit rather than the flat residential re-roof form, and the parish applies the 2021 International Building Code to commercial work. Jefferson Parish's published commercial submittal requirements call for a complete set of plans by a Louisiana registered architect or civil engineer, with a statement of supervision on the first sheet, and the parish publishes no roofing specific exemption from that. It also notes that circumstances particular to an application may dictate deviations from its standard requirements, so the scope is confirmed with the Building Permits Department before anything is filed. Big Easy Roofing runs that process as part of the project.

Can we stay open during the work

Almost always, and on retail, restaurant and medical buildings it is the normal expectation. The roof is worked in sections and left watertight at the end of each day, and noisy operations can be scheduled around trading hours or a delivery window. A tear off is more disruptive than a recover, which is one of the practical arguments for a recover where the code allows one.

Will a recover void my warranty or affect a new one

A recover done to the manufacturer's specification over a compliant substrate can normally carry its own new warranty, including a no dollar limit warranty on qualifying systems. What manufacturers will not warrant is a recover over a substrate that does not meet their conditions, which in practice usually means trapped moisture. That is another reason the moisture survey comes before the quote.

Can we improve the insulation at the same time

Yes, and a re-roof is the natural moment to do it. On a recover, insulation or a cover board can be added into the new build up. On a tear off you get a clean start. Energy code requirements apply to the work, and the payback on the additional insulation is worth running as its own number on a large Metairie roof rather than treating it as an upgrade.

The last contractor quoted a recover without going on the roof

Then the quote is a guess. Whether a recover is even permitted depends on the layer count, the existing covering type and how much of the insulation is wet, and none of those can be established from the ground or from a satellite image. Ask any bidder how they established the layer count and the moisture condition, and treat a route recommended without a survey as an unpriced risk.
