Hammond commercial leak detection at a glance

Water gets into a flat roof at one point and appears inside the building somewhere else entirely, so chasing the stain is how a roof gets repaired repeatedly without the leak stopping. This page is about locating the actual breach, which is a different job from mapping how much insulation is already wet.

What this establishes

The entry point

The breach itself, marked on the roof rather than inferred from where the ceiling is stained. That is what a repair scope needs, because the wet area and the hole are rarely in the same place.

Does a survey need a permit

The standards behind it

ASTM D7877

The standard guide for electronic methods of detecting and locating leaks in waterproof membranes, with ASTM D8231 covering the low voltage scanning practice. Ask which edition a contractor works to, because both have been revised.

What it needs to work

A circuit

An electrically insulating membrane over a conductive earthed substrate, so that current can only pass where there is a hole. Low voltage methods also need the surface wetted; high voltage methods need it dry. Where the assembly cannot provide that, the method cannot read the roof.

The other half of the answer

How much is wet

A different question with different instruments, and the one that decides repair against recover against replacement. It is covered on the commercial roof inspections page rather than repeated here.

The definitive test

A controlled flood

Conclusive on the area it covers, and heavy. It needs a roof that can carry the water, working drains, dams and a plan for getting the water off again, so it suits a defined area rather than a whole roof.

Water entering a flat roof travels. It gets in at a seam, a fastener, a curb or a drain, runs across the deck or sideways inside the insulation, and shows up inside the building at the first joint or low point it reaches, which can be a long way from where it started. That single fact is why a leak gets patched repeatedly at the place the ceiling is stained and carries on leaking.

Electronic leak detection works the other way round. The membrane on a commercial roof is an electrical insulator, so if you put a potential difference across it, current can only flow where the membrane is not continuous. That means a breach announces itself rather than being deduced, which is why the method can be specified against a published standard rather than described in general terms. What it will not tell you is how far the water has already spread inside the assembly, which is the commercial roof inspections question.

The standards behind it, and the roofs it cannot read

Electronic leak detection has published ASTM standards behind the method itself, which is worth knowing because it means the approach can be named and checked. So is the fact that it does not work on every assembly, which is the part a contractor selling the survey tends to leave out.

The method standard

ASTM D7877

The standard guide for electronic methods for detecting and locating leaks in waterproof membranes. ASTM D8231 is the related practice covering low voltage electronic scanning. Both have been revised, so ask which edition is being worked to.

What defeats it

A broken circuit

The method depends on the membrane being an insulator over something conductive and earthed. Conductive components in the build up, ballast, some coatings, and a deck that cannot be earthed will each stop it reading properly. Then a different method is needed.

Why finding the hole is a separate job from mapping the wet

They are different physical questions and they are read by different instruments. The moisture side infers water inside the assembly and tells you how far it has spread; the commercial roof inspections page sets out how that is done. Electronic methods on this page find a discontinuity in the membrane, so they tell you where the water got in.

A roof can carry a large wet area and a breach the size of a fastener head, and a survey that maps the first will not point at the second. Both are worth knowing and they answer different budget questions: the extent decides repair against recover against replacement, and the entry point decides what the repair scope actually says. The extent question is on the commercial roof inspections page.

What a survey should hand you

A marked location for the breach or breaches, photographed, with what was used to find them and under what conditions. If a method was ruled out on your roof, the report should say which one and why, because that is a fact about your assembly that stays useful next time.

Ask for it in writing before the repair is priced. A repair scope written against a marked breach can be checked afterwards. One written against a stain cannot, which is the whole reason the same roof gets repaired again and again.

Why a flat roof here hides its leaks so well

Two conditions in this climate make travelling water harder to trace than the textbook version suggests. Rain arrives hard and briefly, and the roof is warm and wet enough that the building can produce water that looks exactly like a leak and is not one.

Rain arrives in bursts

Storm bands moving up from the coast reach inland Tangipahoa in short heavy bursts rather than steadily. A hole that stays quiet in ordinary weather can pass a lot of water in twenty minutes, which is why some leaks only appear in the heaviest storms and are impossible to reproduce on a dry day.

Water moves sideways before it drops

Insulation boards, the deck flutes and the laps between them all give water somewhere to run before it finds a way through the ceiling. On a large roof the distance between the breach and the stain can be substantial, and it is not predictable from inside, which is why the search has to start on the roof rather than under the mark.

Not everything wet is a leak

In a humid inland climate with air conditioning running, water found on the underside of a deck or near ductwork can be condensation rather than roof water. That is the misdiagnosis that costs the most, because the roof gets repaired, the money is spent and the water keeps appearing. Where the pattern points that way we say so before anybody opens the roof.

Why does my roof keep getting repaired in the same place and still leak

Because the place being repaired is where the water arrives, not where it gets in. Water entering a flat roof runs across the deck or sideways inside the insulation and shows up at the first joint or low point it reaches. Until the breach itself is located, each repair is a guess at the wrong end.

What is the difference between this and a moisture survey

They answer different questions. A moisture survey maps how much of the insulation is already wet, which decides repair against recover against replacement. This finds where the water is getting in, which decides what the repair scope says. Both are useful and the extent question is on the commercial roof inspections page.

How does electronic leak detection actually work

A roofing membrane is an electrical insulator. If a potential difference is applied across it, with something conductive and earthed below, current can only flow where the membrane is not continuous. So a breach reveals itself rather than being deduced, and the operator traces the signal to the exact spot.

Are there standards for it

Yes, which is unusual in this trade. ASTM D7877 is the standard guide for electronic methods for detecting and locating leaks in waterproof membranes, and ASTM D8231 is the standard practice for the low voltage electronic scanning system it describes. Both have been revised, so ask any contractor which edition they work to.

Will it work on my roof

Not necessarily, and anybody who says yes before seeing the roof is guessing. It needs an insulating membrane over a conductive earthed substrate to complete a circuit. Conductive elements in the build up, ballast, some coatings and a deck that cannot be earthed will each stop it reading. That is established before the survey is booked.

What is a flood test and when is it worth it

The area is dammed and deliberately flooded so water finds the breach the way rain would. It is conclusive on the area it covers and it is heavy, so it needs a roof that can carry the water, working drains and a plan for getting the water off. It suits a defined area rather than a whole roof.

My leak only shows up in heavy rain. Is that normal

It is common here. Storm bands moving up from the coast reach inland Tangipahoa in short heavy bursts rather than steadily, so a breach that passes very little water in ordinary weather can pass a lot in twenty minutes. It also makes the leak hard to reproduce on a dry day, which is part of why a method that does not rely on rain is worth using.

Could the water be condensation rather than a leak

It genuinely could, and it is the misdiagnosis that costs the most, because the roof gets repaired and the water keeps appearing. In a humid inland climate with air conditioning running, water on the underside of a deck or near ductwork can be condensation, so it is worth ruling out before anything is opened up.
