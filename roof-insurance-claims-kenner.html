Kenner roof insurance claim documentation at a glance

A homeowner's document trail supports a claim; it does not decide it. What the trail contains, and when each part of it is built, is what this page describes across three folders. The claim decision itself is between the homeowner and the insurer, and this page does not enter into that.

Folder one

Pre-storm baseline

A dated record of the roof before any damage: photographs of every elevation, the year the roof was installed, the material and manufacturer where you have it, and any known existing damage. This folder records the roof's condition on the recorded dates, and it is easiest to build long before the storm.

Folder two

Storm-day record

The event itself and what you did in the hours after it. The date and location of the storm, ideally with a National Weather Service or NOAA storm report; dated photographs of any visible conditions, taken when safe and without delaying urgent protection; and receipts for any immediate mitigation such as tarping. Where a state of emergency has been declared, La. R.S. 29:732 addresses price gouging within the designated area and applicable period; ask for an itemized written invoice that names the work, the address, the date and the amount charged.

Folder three

Claim file

The paper record after the claim is filed: the claim number and the insurer contact, the adjuster inspection date and the notes provided, and a written scope of loss from a roofing contractor against the damage found. Louisiana payment deadlines depend on the statutory category: La. R.S. 22:1892 generally provides 30 days after receipt of satisfactory proofs of loss, while La. R.S. 22:1892.2(A)(2) provides 60 days for catastrophic residential-property losses. Ask your insurer which applies to your claim.

What we do on the trail

Roof survey, photos, written scope

Our part of the trail is a survey of the roof and a written scope of loss against what the survey finds. We photograph the damage, we name the affected materials and quantities, and we deliver the scope in writing. We do not interpret your policy and we do not settle your claim.

What we do not do

Coverage interpretation

La. R.S. 37:2159.1 restricts a roofing contractor from acting as a public adjuster or interpreting insurance policy coverage. What a policy pays, what a deductible should be, whether specific damage is covered, and how a settlement is calculated are all matters for you and your insurer.

The permit is separate

A claim-funded reroof is still a reroof

Where the outcome of a claim is a repair or a reroof, the permit position follows from the work rather than from the funding source. Under La. R.S. 37:3737(I), reroofing on buildings other than one- and two-family dwellings goes to IBC Chapter 15; one- and two-family dwellings go to IRC Chapters 8 and 9 under subsection (I)(2). The application in Kenner is sent to kennerpermits@kenner.la.us or dropped off at 1926 18th Street.

A roof insurance claim is decided by an insurer against a policy the homeowner holds, and the insurer's decision considers many things the homeowner cannot control. The document trail on this page is what the homeowner CAN control: dated records of the roof from different points in time, organized so the homeowner and the insurer can each see what was where and when. A photograph of an intact roof taken a year before the storm takes minutes to file and cannot be reconstructed once the storm has passed.

The trail is the homeowner's. The roofer's part of it is defined and limited: survey the roof, photograph what the survey finds, write a scope of loss against what the survey finds, and, where a declared emergency is in force, deliver mitigation work at a documented, non-gouged price. What that scope translates into on a claim decision is a matter between the homeowner and the insurer, and La. R.S. 37:2159.1 keeps a contractor out of that conversation for good reasons.

The statutes and the permit route

Several Louisiana statutes shape what a roofing contractor can and cannot do around an insurance claim, and the permit position for any repair or reroof follows the work rather than the funding source. All references on this page are to public statute; none of them interprets any specific policy.

Contractor scope

La. R.S. 37:2159.1

Restricts a roofing contractor from acting as a public adjuster or interpreting insurance policy coverage. Our services include a roof survey, dated photographs, a written repair scope, and mitigation when needed. We do not interpret your policy and we do not settle your claim.

Payment deadlines

La. R.S. 22:1892 and 22:1892.2

La. R.S. 22:1892 generally provides a 30-day insurer payment obligation after receipt of satisfactory proofs of loss, subject to the statutory conditions. La. R.S. 22:1892.2(A)(2) provides a 60-day rule for catastrophic residential-property losses. Which deadline applies to any specific claim is a matter for the insurer to determine against the claim record.

Permit route on a claim-funded reroof

IBC Chapter 15 or IRC Chapters 8-9

A reroof funded by a claim is still a reroof, and the permit position follows the work rather than the funding. La. R.S. 37:3737(I)(1) routes buildings other than one- and two-family dwellings to IBC Chapter 15; subsection (I)(2) routes one- and two-family dwellings to IRC Chapters 8 and 9. In Kenner the application is sent to kennerpermits@kenner.la.us or dropped off at the Inspection and Code Enforcement Department at 1926 18th Street, (504) 468-4064.

Emergency pricing

La. R.S. 29:732

Prohibits price gouging during a declared state of emergency, within the designated emergency area and the applicable emergency period. Keep an itemized invoice recording the work, address, date and amount charged. The invoice documents the transaction; it does not by itself establish compliance with the statute.

How the storm-day record actually gets built

Photograph visible conditions when it is safe to do so and without delaying urgent protection. Where mitigation is needed immediately, install it and have the contractor photograph the roof before and after where possible. The homeowner's record does not require perfect ordering; it requires dated evidence of what the roof looked like at each stage.

Retain any itemized invoice for mitigation work alongside the photographs and any weather-event references. That is a mitigation record to keep with your claim documentation. Whether the mitigation cost is reimbursable under a specific policy is a matter between the homeowner and the insurer; the record exists either way.

What a written scope of loss contains

A scope of loss identifies the damaged material, its extent, and the work required to restore it. It names quantities, materials, and any code-required upgrades that follow from the damage. It does not name what a policy pays; that is the insurer's work against the homeowner's policy.

On a claim-funded reroof, the scope also names the permit route the work will engage (IRC Chapters 8-9 for a one- or two-family dwelling, IBC Chapter 15 for other buildings). The application in Kenner is sent to kennerpermits@kenner.la.us or dropped off at 1926 18th Street, and is reviewed at the Permitting Office on (504) 468-4062 option 1.

After a Gulf storm and around the mitigation

The storm-day folder gets built in a short window. Two things reliably help the record: photographs of visible conditions, taken when safe and without delaying urgent protection, and itemized receipts for any mitigation work that names the work, the address, the date and the amount charged.

Photographs when safe, and without delaying protection

Once a tarp is on, the roof underneath is not visible in a photograph, so any pre-tarp photograph has to happen in the short window between the storm ending and the mitigation going down. Where it is safe and does not delay urgent protection, photographs of visible damage from the ground, from an upper window or from a smartphone camera on a pole record what was there.

Where urgent protection cannot wait, install the mitigation and photograph what is accessible before and after. The record does not require perfect ordering; it requires dated evidence of the roof at each stage. Photographing from a safe location without climbing onto the roof is enough for the record; the interior and safety come first.

Itemized receipts for mitigation

A tarp installed in the aftermath of a storm may fall inside a declared emergency period under R.S. 29:732. Ask for an itemized invoice that names the work, the address, the date and the amount charged. The invoice records the transaction; whether the pricing meets the statutory anti-gouging requirement in a specific case is decided against the statute's comparable-market and exception provisions, not by the invoice alone.

Keep the invoice with any timestamp records (a text confirmation, an email, a photograph of the tarp in place taken the day it went down). Those pieces together record when the mitigation happened and at what price; whether the pricing meets the statutory anti-gouging requirement is decided against the statute, not by the invoice on its own.

The permit route on a claim-funded reroof

If the outcome of the claim is a repair or a reroof, the permit for that work is issued by the office covering the specific address. In Kenner that is the City of Kenner Inspection and Code Enforcement Department at 1926 18th Street, (504) 468-4064, with the Permitting Office at (504) 468-4062 option 1 handling the review during business hours. What documents does a roof insurance claim need

Three folders help support a claim: a pre-storm baseline (dated photographs of the intact roof, install date and material, existing damage record), a storm-day record (weather report, dated photographs of visible conditions taken when safe, itemized receipts for any mitigation work), and a claim file (claim number and insurer contact, adjuster inspection date, written scope of loss from a roofing contractor). Whether that documentation is sufficient for a specific claim is between the homeowner and the insurer.

What can I do before a storm

Build folder one. Photograph every elevation on the roof, note the year it was installed and the material, keep the manufacturer warranty document with the paperwork, and update the photographs once a year. A dated pre-storm record of the intact roof takes minutes to build and cannot be reconstructed once the storm has passed.

What do I do in the hours after a storm

When it is safe to do so and without delaying urgent protection, photograph visible conditions before mitigation. Where a leak is active or the building is at further risk, install protection first and photograph what is accessible before and after. Note the storm date and, once safe, retrieve a NOAA or National Weather Service report for the event. Where a state of emergency has been declared, keep an itemized invoice for any mitigation work.

What is a roofing contractor allowed to do on a claim

Our services around a claim include a roof survey, dated photographs, a written repair scope, and mitigation when needed. That is the scope La. R.S. 37:2159.1 leaves to the roofing side.

What is a roofing contractor NOT allowed to do

Interpret your policy, tell you what will be covered, advise on deductibles or settlements, or act as a public adjuster. La. R.S. 37:2159.1 draws that line. Public adjuster licensing is set out in La. R.S. 22:1693 and the standards of conduct in La. R.S. 22:1706, and whether to retain one is a homeowner decision this page does not make.

What is R.S. 22:1892

A Louisiana statute that generally provides a 30-day insurer payment obligation for property claims after receipt of satisfactory proofs of loss, subject to the statutory conditions. A separate provision at La. R.S. 22:1892.2(A)(2) provides a 60-day rule for catastrophic residential-property losses. Which deadline applies to a specific claim is for the insurer to determine.

What is R.S. 29:732

A Louisiana statute prohibiting price gouging during a declared state of emergency, within the designated area and the applicable emergency period. Keep an itemized invoice for any mitigation work. The invoice documents the transaction; whether the pricing meets the statute in a specific case is decided against the statute's comparable-market and exception provisions.

Does a claim-funded reroof need a permit

Yes, on the same terms a self-funded reroof does. La. R.S. 37:3737(I)(1) directs roof construction and reroofing on buildings other than one- and two-family dwellings to IBC Chapter 15; subsection (I)(2) directs one- and two-family dwellings to IRC Chapters 8 and 9. The intake mechanism in Kenner is set out on the Kenner roof replacement page.
