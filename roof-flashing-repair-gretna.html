Where flashing sits in the code

IRC Chapter 9

Act 239 of 2025 sends a one or two family dwelling to International Residential Code Chapters 8 and 9. Chapter 9 is the roof assemblies chapter, which is where flashing requirements live, so on a permitted reroof the flashing is inside the scope of the code the work is judged against.

The rule that matters

Metal laps, sealant does not

Flashing works by overlapping pieces of metal so water is always directed onto the next surface down. Sealant is what finishes a correct detail, not what makes one. A bead of mastic over a gap is a repair with a service life measured in seasons.

Flashing is the least glamorous part of a roof and the part that decides whether the rest of it works. A field of shingles is simply a sloped surface shedding water downhill, which is a problem already solved. Every difficulty a roof has is at the places where that surface is interrupted: a chimney coming through it, a wall running into it, two slopes meeting, a pipe, a skylight, an edge. Flashing is the metal that manages water at each of those, and a leak on an otherwise sound roof is nearly always at one of them.

The principle behind every type is the same and it is worth understanding, because it is how you tell a real repair from a cosmetic one. Pieces of metal are overlapped so that water running down always lands on top of the next piece rather than behind it, in the same way roof tiles or shingles work. Nothing in a correct flashing detail depends on a sealant staying stuck. Sealant has a place, at a termination or a fastener head, but where it is doing the actual work of keeping water out, the detail underneath it is wrong and the clock is running.

One question to ask about flashing before you buy a new roof

Flashing outlives a roof covering, which is exactly why it gets left in place when the covering above it is replaced, and why a new roof can leak in its first year at a detail nobody touched. One question at quote stage settles it, and it costs nothing to ask.

Ask whether the flashings are new or reused

And get it in writing

A reroof quote that does not say is hiding the largest quality variable in the job. New step flashing laced into new courses costs more than lifting the covering around metal that is already there, and the difference does not show on the finished roof.

Reused flashing is not automatically wrong

It is a decision, not a default

Sound counter flashing let properly into masonry can reasonably stay. What should not happen is that nobody decides, and it stays because lifting it is work. Ask which pieces are being replaced and why, and the answer tells you a lot about the contractor.

Why sealant keeps being used where flashing is needed

Because it works, briefly, and it is quick. A bead of mastic over a failed junction will stop a leak, and it will stop it for long enough that the person who applied it is not there when it comes back. It also hides the detail underneath, so the next person to look has to remove it before they can see anything.

The tell on an inspection is a flashing that has been sealed more than once, in layers. That is not a maintained roof, it is a detail that has been failing for years, and it usually means the metal underneath needs to come out rather than be sealed again.

Chimneys are where two trades meet and neither owns it

A chimney junction needs step flashing laced into the roof covering and counter flashing let into a mortar joint above it, so that water coming down the masonry is thrown out over the step flashing rather than behind it. That is a roofing detail and a masonry detail in the same six inches.

When counter flashing is surface sealed to the brick instead of being cut in, it holds for a while and then lets go, and the leak turns up beside the chimney rather than at it. Repointing that is failing at the same time is worth dealing with in the same visit, and on a historic district address the appearance of that masonry is exactly what the Commission is reviewing.

The kick out flashing and the wall nobody looks at

If there is one detail worth walking outside and checking on your own house, it is this one. It is small, it is cheap, it is frequently absent altogether, and when it is absent the damage does not appear on the roof or the ceiling but inside the wall.

What it is and where to look

Where a roof slope ends at a wall that keeps going, usually where a garage or an addition meets the main house, the last piece of step flashing at the bottom should be bent outward to throw water clear of the wall and into the gutter. That bent piece is the kick out, and you can see it from the ground at the point where the roof edge meets the wall above the gutter.

If the step flashing simply stops at the gutter with nothing turning the water out, every bit of water running down that junction is being delivered to the top of the wall cladding instead of to the gutter.

Why it does so much damage so quietly

Water behind cladding has no route back out and nothing to evaporate it. It sits against sheathing and framing, and in a climate this humid it stays wet rather than drying out between rains. Nothing drips inside because the water never reaches a ceiling.

By the time it shows, as paint failing, a soft patch of siding, or a stain appearing well below the roof line, it has been going on for a long time. That is why a missing kick out gets blamed on the siding, the windows or the paint rather than on the roof.

It sits in the lowest band on this page

Fitting one is a small piece of formed metal and lifting a few courses of covering, so it sits in the same $350 to $700 band as any other single detail rather than needing a figure of its own. There is little that waiting improves, and related damage tends to spread rather than stop.

Where a roof is being replaced anyway, it should simply be part of the work, and it is worth naming it on the quote so that it is. It is exactly the kind of detail that is omitted without anybody deciding to omit it.

What is roof flashing

The metal that manages water everywhere the roof covering is interrupted: at a wall, a chimney, a valley, a pipe, a skylight or an edge. It works by overlapping so that water running down always lands on top of the next piece rather than behind it. Nothing in a correct detail depends on a sealant staying stuck.

Can flashing just be sealed instead of replaced

It can be, and it will stop the leak for a while. What it will not do is fix the detail, and it hides the metal underneath so the next person has to strip the sealant before they can see anything. Flashing that has been sealed in layers over the years is a detail that needs replacing rather than sealing again.

What is a kick out flashing and do I have one

It is the piece at the bottom of a roof to wall junction, bent outward to throw water into the gutter instead of down the wall. You can check from the ground at the point where a roof edge meets a wall that carries on past it, typically where a garage or addition meets the house. If the step flashing just stops, there is no kick out.

What happens if the kick out is missing

Water runs down inside the wall rather than into the gutter, and it stays there because it has no route out and little chance to dry in this climate. Nothing drips inside, so it shows up late as failing paint, soft siding or a stain well below the roof line, and it usually gets blamed on the siding rather than the roof.

Why does my roof leak at the chimney

A chimney needs step flashing laced into the covering and counter flashing let into a mortar joint above it.
