Why Metairie roofs wear out early

Metairie sits low, flat and unshaded across most of its subdivisions, which is a harder brief for a roof than it sounds. The open canopy that keeps the streets bright also means the shingle field takes direct sun from dawn to dusk with nothing breaking it up, and asphalt ages on heat and ultraviolet before it ages on anything else. Add the humidity coming off Lake Pontchartrain and the seasonal wind events, and a roof rated for thirty years in a temperate state is doing well to see twenty here.

The second factor is history. A large share of Metairie housing stock was re roofed in a hurry in the years after Katrina, and a lot of that work went on as a second layer over the existing shingles rather than a full strip. That was legal, it was faster, and it is now the single most common surprise we find when we open a roof up. A second layer traps heat against the deck, hides the condition of the plywood underneath, and doubles what it costs to take off when the roof finally goes.

What the parish requires before we start

Metairie is unincorporated, so your roof is permitted by Jefferson Parish and not by the City of New Orleans. People get this wrong constantly, and it matters, because the parish process is genuinely different and unusually light touch.

The four Jefferson Parish rules that apply to your roof

  • The permit is $50. A flat residential re roofing permit fee, paid by credit card through the parish MGO Connect portal. No construction plans and no survey are required.
  • Work over $7,500 needs a licensed contractor. From 1 January 2026, residential roof work valued above $7,500 requires a Louisiana Residential Roofing or Residential Construction licence. That threshold catches almost every full replacement.
  • The 2021 International Residential Code governs the work. It sets the nailing pattern, the underlayment and the edge detail your roof has to be built to.
  • No inspector visits the site. Jefferson Parish signs the job off from geo tagged photographs of every phase, with the nailing pattern visible and the material packaging showing its wind rating.

Source: Jefferson Parish residential re roofing permit requirements, jeffparish.gov

That last rule is the one worth reading twice. Because nobody from the parish stands on your roof, the photographic record is the inspection. If a contractor is casual about taking it, you have no evidence the roof was built to code, and that becomes your problem at resale or at claim time rather than theirs.

What it costs, and what moves the number

Most full architectural asphalt replacements in Metairie land between $7,000 and $18,000. Larger footprints, steep or complex roofs, and standing seam metal systems run from $16,000 up to $28,000. The spread inside those ranges is not vagueness, it is four or five specific variables, and a decent quote names all of them.

Tear off layers is the big one, at roughly $900 to $2,400 of difference. Decking replacement is the one nobody can price honestly until the old roof is off, so it should appear in your scope as a per sheet rate, around $65 to $95, rather than a guess. A FORTIFIED upgrade adds somewhere between $1,800 and $4,500 and buys you a sealed roof deck, ring shank nails and enhanced edge metal.

The state grant, described honestly

The Louisiana Fortify Homes Program awards up to $10,000 toward bringing a roof to the FORTIFIED standard. Two things about it get misrepresented in local roofing advertising and both are worth knowing before you plan around it.

First, it pays for the FORTIFIED upgrade portion of the work, not for your whole roof. If the job is $20,000 and the FORTIFIED element of it is $4,000, the grant addresses the $4,000. Second, it runs as a lottery with limited application windows rather than as an entitlement you can simply claim. It is worth applying for. It is not worth building your budget on before you have been awarded it.

Where we work in Metairie

The roof stock changes noticeably across the parish, and so does what fails first on it.

Old Metairie

Older, larger homes with complex hip roofs, real chimneys and more flashing detail than anywhere else in the parish. Leaks usually start at a transition, not in the field.

Bucktown and Lakeview edge

Close to the lake, so wind driven rain at the rake and eave edges is the recurring problem. Edge metal quality matters more here than shingle brand.

Airline and Veterans corridor

Post war subdivisions with simple gable roofs, and the highest concentration of double layer shingle work we find anywhere in Metairie.

Elmwood and River Ridge line

Mixed residential and light commercial, so we run both shingle and low slope systems on the same street, often on the same week.

Metairie roof replacement questions

How much does a new roof cost in Metairie

Most full architectural asphalt replacements land between $7,000 and $18,000 depending on size, pitch and how many layers come off. Larger or complex roofs and standing seam metal run $16,000 to $28,000. The Jefferson Parish permit adds a flat $50.

Do I need a permit to replace a roof in Metairie

Yes. Metairie sits in unincorporated Jefferson Parish, which requires a residential re roofing permit. It is $50, paid by card through the parish MGO Connect portal, with no construction plans and no survey required.

Will an inspector come to my house

No. The parish verifies re roofing from geo tagged photographs of every phase rather than a site visit, with the nailing pattern visible and the material packaging showing its wind rating.

Can I get the $10,000 state grant

The Louisiana Fortify Homes Program awards up to $10,000, but it pays for the FORTIFIED upgrade portion of the work rather than the whole roof, and it runs as a lottery with limited windows.

How do I know whether there are two layers up there

From the ground you often cannot. The tell is at the rake edge, where a double layer sits visibly proud of the fascia. If your house was re roofed between 2006 and 2010, assume two layers until an inspection says otherwise.
