St. Rose commercial roof replacement at a glance

A commercial reroof in St. Rose may involve three key references: the parish commercial permitting guide, the regional commission's roof covering handout, and R.S. 37:3737, under which a parish that issues construction permits must permit and inspect roofing on buildings other than one and two family dwellings under International Building Code Chapter 15. The parish confirms which items apply.

The parish has a commercial guide

Flowchart and permitting guide

The parish publishes a Commercial Permitting Flowchart and Guide that starts with a pre-application meeting through Planning and Zoning on (985) 783-5060. It describes commercial permitting in general, so which items a reroof needs is for the permit officers.

Fire Marshal letter in the guide

Listed, not confirmed for a reroof

The guide lists "A Fire Marshal approval letter" and says it determines that the plans of the building will meet their requirements for approval. Whether a reroof needs one is for the permit officers to say.

Health Department letter in the guide

Where applicable, per the guide

The guide lists a Department of Health approval letter "when applicable", and says it is necessary when the project will be providing food to the public.

Two parish pages, two sets of numbers

So we print neither

The parish's new construction page for commercial and industrial buildings lists the same two approvals with different contact numbers from the guide's. Ask Planning and Zoning which to call.

The regional handout has a commercial paragraph

Version of 10 October 2025

The South Central commission's roof covering handout, which the parish permits page links and which is headed for areas where wind design is required, says that for commercial roofs on slopes from 2:12 up to 4:12 underlayment is two layers, and on slopes of 4:12 or greater one layer with laps of not less than 4 inches. Its wording puts 4:12 in both ranges, so we confirm which applies. For flat roof systems it says to comply with the noted code references and the manufacturer's instructions.

Recover has limits

In the same handout

The handout says new roof coverings shall not be installed without first removing all existing layers where the roof has two or more applications of coverings, and that attaching a new covering to existing wood shake, slate, clay, cement or asbestos-cement tile is not allowed.

This page addresses roofing on buildings other than one- and two-family dwellings. For a mixed use building, confirm with the parish which rules apply before scoping.

Our practice is to price commercial replacement on the assembly and the access rather than from a published range, so call Big Easy Roofing for a scoped price on your St. Rose building.

What the parish guide lists and the regional handout sets

State law requires a parish that issues construction permits to permit and inspect buildings other than one and two family dwellings for roof construction and reroofing under International Building Code Chapter 15. The parish commercial guide covers commercial permitting, and the regional handout, headed for areas where wind design is required, describes two inspection options. The parish confirms what applies.

State law

R.S. 37:3737(I)(1)

The commission's compilation prints it: a municipality or parish that issues a permit for construction shall do all of the following, starting with: "Permit and inspect all buildings, other than one- and two-family dwellings, for roof construction and reroofing in compliance with the International Building Code Chapter 15 requirements as adopted and promulgated by the commission." The State Licensing Board says permitting and inspection of reroofing is required for projects beginning on or after 1 August 2025.

The guide's first step

A pre-application meeting

The guide says a meeting with wastewater, waterworks, MS4, planning and drainage staff is scheduled to help answer any questions or concerns about the project. It is requested through Planning and Zoning on (985) 783-5060.

Inspection, option one

On site, scheduled next business day

The handout says the building department inspects each roof section before the covering is applied, that inspections are scheduled by calling 985-655-1070, and that "Inspections are scheduled for the next business day."

Inspection, option two

Geotagged photographs

In lieu of on-site inspections the handout points to a separate sheet for submitting geotagged photographs through an online document portal. R.S. 37:3737(H) says the building official "may accept" location-verified photographs or videos for required roofing or reroofing inspections, so whether they are accepted for a given job is for the parish to say.

What we put in the file

Our practice, not a parish rule: for a reroof we prepare a roof plan showing drains, curbs and penetrations, with the new assembly described from deck to membrane, in paper and digital form.

Separately, the commercial guide lists two sets of paper construction plans with a digital copy as a submittal item. Which plans a reroof needs is for the permit officers, and building code questions go to a South Central Development plan reviewer, as the guide directs.

What we do not say

We do not say a reroof needs the Fire Marshal letter or the Health Department letter. The guide and the new construction page describe commercial permitting, and the permit officers say which items a reroof needs.

We do not say which inspection route the parish will accept for a given job. We ask, and write the answer into the scope.

Replacing a roof over a building that stays open

Where a commercial building keeps trading while the roof comes off, the sequence matters as much as the assembly. These are the things we plan with the building before work starts, and the record we keep, which follows the handout's own photograph checklist.

Phasing around the occupier

A reroof over a shop floor, a kitchen or an office is planned section by section. Our practice is to plan each day's work so the roof is watertight again at the end of it, weather permitting.

Where a notice to the occupier, a unit shutdown or a crane slot is needed, it is scheduled with the building rather than assumed.

Drains, falls and insulation together

A replacement can be the point at which drainage, falls and insulation are corrected together, depending on the assembly and the scope.

In our experience ponding can shorten the life of a membrane, so drainage is settled with the assembly rather than after it.

The record we keep

The handout's photograph checklist asks for four photographs of decking where the decking is new, four of underlayment and six of roofing material, including the fastener package and label, the installed fastener pattern and a wide angle of the finished roof with its accessories.

Our practice is to keep a record like that whichever inspection route is used, using the handout's checklist as our model, with the permit and its inspections, and to hand it over with the scope.

Who issues the commercial roofing permit in St. Rose

St. Charles Parish. St. Rose is unincorporated and the parish has no incorporated cities, so Planning and Zoning on (985) 783-5060 is the office to ask.

What code does a commercial reroof answer to

For buildings other than one- and two-family dwellings, R.S. 37:3737(I)(1) says a municipality or parish that issues a permit for construction shall permit and inspect roof construction and reroofing in compliance with International Building Code Chapter 15 requirements as adopted and promulgated by the commission.

Does a commercial reroof need a Fire Marshal letter

The parish commercial permitting guide lists a Fire Marshal approval letter. Whether a reroof needs one is for the permit officers to say. We do not print a contact number, because the guide and the parish new construction page list different ones.

Does it need a Health Department letter

The guide lists one "when applicable" and says it is necessary when the project will be providing food to the public. Ask the permit officers whether it applies to your building.

Can the new roof go over the old one

The regional handout says all existing layers come off first where there are two or more coverings, and that attaching a new covering to existing wood shake, slate, clay, cement or asbestos-cement tile is not allowed. For a single covering of another type the handout is silent, so we ask the building official before specifying a recover.

How is a commercial reroof inspected

The handout describes two options: an on-site inspection of each roof section before the covering goes on, scheduled for the next business day, or geotagged photographs submitted in place of it. R.S. 37:3737(H) says the building official may accept photographs, so whether they are accepted for a given job is the parish's decision, and we ask which route it will accept for yours.

Does my roofer need a state licence for a commercial replacement

Commercial work carries its own licence threshold, set by the State Licensing Board for Contractors at fifty thousand dollars for property used for commercial purposes (the Board confirms how it is measured), and it is separate from the residential roofing classification that applies to houses. Ask for the Louisiana state licence number and its expiry date and verify both directly with the Board.

Can my roofer act as my public adjuster

La. R.S. 22:1706(H)(11) bars a public adjuster from acting as a contractor or providing roofing or repair services to the insured on a claim they have processed. On the roofer side, our practice is to document the physical condition of the roof and give an itemised estimate of the work; coverage questions go to the insurer or to a licensed public adjuster.

Survey the roof, ask the parish before any file is lodged

We survey the roof, decide tear off or recover on what we find, and put the parish questions to Planning and Zoning before the file is lodged. If you would rather start in writing, tell us what the job involves and we will come back with an estimate.
