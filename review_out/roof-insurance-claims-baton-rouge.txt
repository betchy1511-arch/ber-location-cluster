Baton Rouge roof insurance claims
at a glance
This page is about paperwork rather than about cover, and that is deliberate. A roofing contractor in Louisiana may document, itemise and price damage. Telling you what your policy does is a job for somebody else, and the law says so.

What we will not do
Interpret your policy
La. R.S. 37:2159.1 limits what a roofing contractor may tell a homeowner about their own insurance cover. We keep to it. Coverage is a question for your insurer, and anyone answering it confidently on your doorstep is either guessing or should not be.
The other half of that rule
R.S. 22:1706(H)(11)
A licensed public adjuster is barred from acting as a contractor or providing roofing or repair services on a claim they have processed. Louisiana separates the two roles from both directions, which is worth knowing when somebody offers to do both.
The document you should not sign
An assignment
Anything that assigns your claim, your benefits or your right to proceeds to a contractor hands away the part of this you control. Get the price in writing with a date on it instead.

The most useful thing a roofing page can tell a Baton Rouge homeowner about an insurance claim is which questions to ask whom. There are two separate jobs in a roof claim and Louisiana keeps them apart on purpose. One is establishing what is damaged and what it costs to put right, which is a roofing question. The other is establishing what the policy responds to, which is an insurance question. A contractor answering the second one is doing something the state has legislated against.
That separation is the reason this page reads differently from every other roof insurance page in this market. It is not that we know less. It is that a roofer who tells you confidently that your damage is covered has told you something they are not in a position to know and not permitted to say, and the moment you rely on it, you are the one carrying the risk. What we can do is make the roofing side of it so well documented that nobody has to take anybody's word for anything.

What a documented roof scope
actually contains
A scope worth having can be read by you, by your insurer, by another roofer or by anybody else you choose to show it to. That is a higher bar than a quote, and it is entirely within what a contractor is allowed to produce.
Photographs taken before anything moves
Time and location stamped
Every damaged element photographed where it is, from the ground and from the roof, before any tarping, clearing or repair. Once something has been moved or made safe, that record cannot be recreated.
Each item named separately
Not one storm total
Covering, flashing, valley, ridge, gutter, vent, decking, each named, each described, each with a price against it and a photograph of the thing being claimed. A single figure with nothing behind it is not comparable to anything.
Where repair stops and replacement starts
Said plainly
A scope should state the point at which the job stops being a repair, and why. Damage across several slopes on an older covering is a different proposition from damage in one place, and the reasoning belongs in writing rather than in a conversation.
And the permit, which is new
Since 1 August 2025
A full reroof in East Baton Rouge now needs its own permit and inspection under La. R.S. 40:1730.23(K), with the parish photograph set filed through MyGovernmentOnline. That is a real cost and a real step in the programme, and it belongs on the scope rather than appearing later.
Why we photograph before we tarp
After a storm the instinct is to get the roof covered, and that instinct is correct. The only thing that has to happen first is the photographs, because a tarp hides exactly the evidence somebody will later want to see.
It takes minutes. Our emergency work is documented in that order for that reason: photograph, then make safe, then repair. The pack is yours whether or not you go on to use us for the work.
Two different kinds of damage need two different records
Wind and hail break a roof differently and are documented differently. Wind breaks the seal strip that bonds each shingle course to the one below, so the course lies back down looking untouched. Hail fractures the mat and strips granules in defined patches, and is counted in a marked test square rather than judged by eye.
A roof can carry both. Where it does, each is recorded separately rather than rolled into one total, because they are different defects with different repairs. The storm damage and hail pages set out each method in full.

Before you sign anything
a roofer puts in front of you
Storm work brings people to the door, and some of them were not in Baton Rouge the week before. Four checks cost nothing and take a few minutes, and they are the difference between a contractor you can hold to something and one you cannot find afterwards.
Check the licence, on the board rather than on the van
Louisiana licenses contractors through the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors, and the board is where a licence is verified rather than on a business card or a truck door. Ask for the name the licence is held in, which is not always the name on the paperwork you are being handed.
If the answer is vague, that is the answer. A licensed contractor has no difficulty telling you exactly what they are licensed as.
Never sign an assignment of your claim or benefits
This is the single most consequential piece of paper in a roof claim. A document that assigns your claim, your benefits or your right to proceeds hands the contractor the part of the process you would otherwise control, and it is often presented as a formality so that work can start quickly.
You do not need to sign anything like it to get a roof documented or repaired. A dated written price for described work is what a straightforward job runs on. If a signature is being pressed on the doorstep, that pressure is itself the information.
Be wary of an offer to handle your deductible
An offer to absorb, waive or take care of your deductible is a reason to stop rather than a discount, and it tends to arrive alongside an assurance about what is covered, which is the thing a contractor is not permitted to give you in the first place.
The same goes for a price that moves depending on what the claim pays rather than on what the work is. Ask what the work costs. That question has one answer and it does not depend on your policy.

Will you handle my insurance claim
No, and be wary of a roofer who says they will. La. R.S. 37:2159.1 limits what a roofing contractor may tell a homeowner about their own cover. What we do is photograph the damage, itemise it, price it and hand you the pack, which is the material an insurer needs from a roofer. The claim itself stays yours.
What is a public adjuster and do I need one
A public adjuster is a separate profession licensed by the Louisiana Department of Insurance to represent a policyholder on a claim. Whether you need one is your decision and not a roofing question. What is worth knowing is that R.S. 22:1706(H)(11) bars a public adjuster from acting as a contractor or doing roofing work on a claim they have processed.
Why will you not tell me if my damage is covered
Because Louisiana law restricts what a roofing contractor may tell a homeowner about their own insurance cover, and because we would not actually know. Cover turns on the wording of your policy, which we have not read and are not qualified to construe. Your insurer can answer it and we can make sure the roofing side is documented properly.
What should a roof scope for a claim contain
Each damaged element named separately, described, priced, and photographed, with the photographs taken before anything was moved. It should also say plainly where repair stops and replacement starts, and include the permit where a reroof is involved. A single line and a single number is not something anyone can check.
Should I sign an assignment of benefits
Not without advice you trust, and you do not need to sign one to have a roof documented or repaired. A document assigning your claim, your benefits or your right to proceeds hands away the part of the process you control. A dated written price for described work is what a straightforward job runs on.
A roofer offered to cover my deductible, is that normal
It is a reason to stop rather than a discount, and it usually arrives with an assurance about what is covered, which is the thing a contractor is not permitted to give you. Ask instead what the work costs. That question has one answer and it does not change depending on your policy.
What does the work itself cost
Our estimated band for a residential roof repair in Baton Rouge is $350 to $1,500, and for a full asphalt replacement $7,000 to $18,000. Those are the same figures as on our repairs and replacement pages, and they are estimates rather than quotes. The permit is a separate cost and it is on the scope.
Does a claim funded reroof still need a permit
Yes. Act 239 of 2025 added La. R.S. 40:1730.23(K), which requires a permit and an inspection on roof construction and reroofing regardless of who is paying for it. East Baton Rouge verifies it from a set of 16 geotagged photographs filed through MyGovernmentOnline, so the photography has to start before the tear off does.
Who issues the permit if I am not in Baton Rouge proper
The address decides it. Permitting inside the City of St. George limits transferred to St. George for applications filed on or after 16 October 2024, and Baker, Zachary and Central are separate municipalities within the parish. It is the first thing we check rather than an afterthought.
