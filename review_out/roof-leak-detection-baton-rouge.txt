A leak has two locations and people naturally only know about one of them. There is the place water gets into the roof and the place it eventually falls out of the ceiling, and between them there can be several feet of travel along a rafter or across the back of the decking. This is why patching the ceiling above the stain is the one repair that reliably does not work, and why a roofer who quotes over the phone from a description of the stain is guessing.
Detection is a process of elimination worked in a fixed order, from the things that fail most readily to the things that fail rarely. Penetrations first, because every pipe, vent and chimney is a hole somebody made in a watertight surface. Then the changes of direction, valleys and wall abutments, where water concentrates and where flashing does the work. Then the field of the roof itself, which is the last suspect rather than the first, because a plain slope of shingles is the part of a roof least likely to fail on its own.
Where roof leaks actually start
in the order we check them
Leaks are not evenly distributed across a roof. They cluster at the places where something interrupts the covering, which is why the search runs in this order rather than starting with the shingles. The order matters as much as the list, because a roof condemned before its details have been cleared may never have needed replacing.

Before anything else, is it
actually a leak
This is the question that saves the most money on a Gulf coast house and the one almost no roofing page asks. Water found in an attic here is not automatically rainwater, and the difference decides whether you need a roofer at all.
Condensation looks exactly like a leak
From below
Warm damp air reaching a cold surface leaves water on it. In an air conditioned house in a humid climate that surface can be the underside of the decking, a duct, or the metal of a vent, and what you see downstairs is a damp patch on a ceiling.
The tell is the weather it appears in
Not the amount of water
A leak follows rain. Condensation follows temperature and humidity, so it turns up in dry weather, on cool nights after a hot day, and it tracks how hard the air conditioning is working. If the damp appears when it has not rained, the roof is not the suspect.
Where it usually comes from
Air escaping upward
Gaps around light fittings, loft hatches and pipework let conditioned air into the attic, and a bathroom or dryer duct that terminates in the attic puts warm wet air there directly. The roof is the surface it condenses on rather than the source.
Why it matters commercially
A sound roof gets replaced
This is the misdiagnosis that costs the most, because the roof is replaced, the money is spent, and the water keeps appearing. We check it before quoting any roofing work, and where the answer is condensation we will say so and point you at ventilation instead.
What water testing is, and when it is worth doing
Where the trace narrows the field to two or three candidates but nothing is visibly wrong, the roof is wetted deliberately, one small area at a time, starting low and working up, with somebody inside watching for the drip to start. It is slow on purpose. Soaking a whole roof at once tells you there is a leak, which you already knew.
It is worth doing on an intermittent leak, on a roof where the obvious suspects have been cleared, and before anyone recommends a replacement on a covering that otherwise looks serviceable. It is the difference between knowing and believing.
The leak that only happens sometimes
An intermittent leak is not a mystery, it is a clue. Water needs a particular set of conditions to reach the inside, and the conditions tell you what the fault is. Rain from one direction only points at a wall abutment or a detail on that elevation. Rain lasting hours points at saturation finding a slow path. A short violent downpour points at capacity, which is drainage rather than covering.
So the useful thing to record before anyone visits is not how much water came in. It is when it came in, how long it had been raining, and which way the wind was blowing. That narrows the search before anyone is on the roof.

What a Baton Rouge climate
does to leak hunting
Heat, humidity and heavy rainfall change which faults appear on a roof here and which diagnostic shortcuts stop working altogether. Three things about this climate matter more in Baton Rouge than they would in a drier one, and each of them changes where a leak hunt starts rather than merely how long it takes.
Rain arrives fast, which tests capacity rather than covering
Rainfall here comes in short intense bursts as well as long soaking days, and the two produce different failures. Intense rain overwhelms drainage, so water backs up above the covering, gets under it at the edge and appears inside without anything on the roof being broken at all.
That is why a leak that only appears in the heaviest rain, on a roof with no visible defect, is a drainage question before it is a roofing one. Our gutter pages cover the capacity side of that in full.
Humidity makes condensation a live suspect all year
In a dry climate you can reasonably assume water in an attic came from outside. Here you cannot. The gap between indoor and outdoor conditions is large for most of the year, and anywhere warm damp air meets a cooler surface is a candidate.
Practically, that means the attic gets inspected as part of leak detection rather than only the roof, and the first question asked is whether the damp has ever appeared when it has not been raining.
Sun ages the small rubber parts fastest
The component on a roof with the shortest life is generally the rubber collar on a pipe boot, and ultraviolet exposure is what finishes it. It is a small part, it splits rather than wears away, and it sits directly above a hole through the deck.
It is the first thing checked for a reason: it is the cheapest thing on the roof to put right and one of the more common single causes of a ceiling stain on an otherwise sound roof.

Why can you not just patch the ceiling where the stain is
Because water travels before it drips. It runs down rafters and across the back of the decking and falls through at the first opportunity, so the stain marks the end of the journey rather than the start. Repairing the roof directly above a stain is the one fix that reliably does not work.
What is water testing
Wetting the roof deliberately, one small area at a time, starting low and working up, with somebody inside watching for the drip. It is deliberately slow, because soaking the whole roof at once only confirms there is a leak. It is worth doing on an intermittent leak or before anyone recommends replacing a roof that otherwise looks serviceable.
Where do you look first
Penetrations, because every pipe, vent and chimney is a hole made in a watertight surface. Then valleys and wall flashings, where water concentrates. The plain field of the roof is the last suspect rather than the first, because a slope of shingles is the part least likely to fail on its own.
My ceiling is damp but it has not rained, what is that
That is the single most useful thing you can tell a roofer, and it usually points away from the roof. Damp that appears in dry weather is condensation rather than a leak: warm humid air reaching a cooler surface such as the underside of the decking or a duct. Replacing the roof will not stop it.
How do you tell condensation from a leak
By what it correlates with. A leak follows rain and stops when the roof dries. Condensation follows temperature and humidity, appears on cool nights after hot days, and tracks how hard the air conditioning is working. We check the attic and take the history before quoting any roofing work.
If it is condensation, what fixes it
Air sealing and ventilation rather than roofing. Gaps around light fittings and loft hatches let conditioned air into the attic, and a bathroom or dryer duct terminating in the attic puts warm wet air there directly. Our Baton Rouge roof ventilation page covers the intake and exhaust side of it.
My leak only happens in certain rain, is that harder to find
It is actually easier, because the conditions narrow the fault. Rain from one direction only points at a detail on that elevation. Rain lasting hours points at saturation finding a slow path. A short violent downpour points at drainage capacity rather than the covering. Note when it happens and which way the wind was blowing.
Does finding a leak mean I need a new roof
Not on its own, and we will not tell you it does before the details have been eliminated. Pipe boots, flashings and valleys are repairable on a roof that is otherwise sound. A replacement is the answer when the covering itself has reached the end of its life, and that is a conclusion reached after the search rather than before it.
Does leak work need a permit in East Baton Rouge
Finding a leak does not, and neither does repairing it, because a repair is not the reroofing that La. R.S. 40:1730.23(K) names. A full replacement does need a permit and an inspection, through East Baton Rouge Permits and Inspections or through St. George, Baker, Zachary or Central depending on the address.
