LaPlace commercial re-roofing at a glance

A commercial re-roof sets how the new roof resists wind. International Building Code Chapter 15 asks for roof coverings designed for wind loads and, on membrane roofs, tested to a named standard. The parish Hazard Mitigation Plan lists materials rated for the appropriate windspeeds as a measure for future codes, recorded as not started.

Wind is already in the code

IBC Section 1504

Section 1504.1 says roof decks and roof coverings shall be designed for wind loads in accordance with Chapter 16 and Sections 1504.2, 1504.3, 1504.4 and 1504.5. Section 1512.1 applies Chapter 15 to the materials and methods used for recovering or replacing an existing roof covering.

Membranes are tested

FM 4474, UL 580 or UL 1897

Section 1504.4.1 says built-up, modified bitumen, fully adhered or mechanically attached single-ply roof systems, metal panel roof systems applied to a solid or closely fitted deck and other types of membrane roof coverings shall be tested in accordance with FM 4474, UL 580 or UL 1897.

The edge has its own test

ANSI/SPRI ES-1

Section 1504.6 covers metal edge systems, except gutters and counterflashing, on built-up, modified bitumen and single-ply roofs below a 2:12 slope. They are tested under Test Methods RE-1, RE-2 and RE-3 of ANSI/SPRI ES-1.

The parish plan names it

Mitigation Action 2

The parish Hazard Mitigation Plan lists "using materials rated for the appropriate windspeeds" among the measures for future building codes and ordinances covering industrial, commercial and residential structures. The plan records that action as not started.

Which office covers you

St. John the Baptist Parish

A LaPlace commercial re-roof is filed with the Permit Division on (985) 651-5565 at 1811 W. Airline Highway in LaPlace, through MyGovernmentOnline or in person.

What Chapter 15 asks of a re-roofed low slope assembly

The wind section of Chapter 15 does not treat a low slope roof as one thing. The field of the membrane, the metal edge, a gutter that holds the membrane edge and a ballasted system each have their own requirement. The table sets out what the code text says for each and what goes in our scope.

The code and the parish file, on a LaPlace commercial re-roof

Louisiana requires every parish or municipality that issues construction permits to permit and inspect reroofing, and for buildings other than one and two family dwellings the code named is International Building Code Chapter 15. Section 1512.1 brings a recover or replacement under the whole chapter, the wind section included. The parish Permit Division confirms what the scope requires.

The permit statute

R.S. 37:3737(I)

Act 239 of 2025 required any parish or municipality that issues construction permits to permit and inspect roof construction and reroofing. Act 881 of 2026 recodified the section. Subsection (I)(1) names IBC Chapter 15 for buildings other than one and two family dwellings.

Reroofing answers to the chapter

IBC Section 1512.1

Section 1512.1 says: "Materials and methods of application used for recovering or replacing an existing roof covering shall comply with the requirements of Chapter 15." The wind resistance requirements in Section 1504 sit inside that chapter.

Who confirms the scope

St. John the Baptist Parish

The Permit Division on (985) 651-5565, on the 1st floor of the parish Government Building at 1811 W. Airline Highway in LaPlace. Applications go through MyGovernmentOnline or in person at the counter.

What the file shows

The assembly by name

Our practice, not a parish rule: the scope we file names the membrane assembly, its fastening or adhesive pattern and the edge system, each with the standard it was tested to, so the plan set and the roof describe the same thing.

Why the edge gets its own line

Section 1504.6 gives metal edge systems on low slope roofs their own design and test requirement, apart from the membrane in the field. The edge is where the membrane is terminated and held.

Our reading, not the code's: because the code tests the edge separately, a re-roof scope that names only the membrane has left out part of what the code asks for. We name both.

Where tear off or recover is decided

This page does not restate the recover rules. They sit on the commercial roof replacement page, with the Section 1512 bar and the parish commercial form that the file is built on.

Either way the answer comes out, the new covering answers to Chapter 15, and that is the point of Section 1512.1.

What the parish plan says about wind and roofs

The St. John the Baptist Parish Hazard Mitigation Plan, updated in 2021, says damage from high winds can include roof damage and the destruction of homes and commercial buildings. It proposes codes that use materials rated for the appropriate windspeeds, and it records that action as carried over and not started.

What the plan says wind does

The plan says "Damage from high winds can include roof damage, destruction of homes and commercial buildings, ...". It adds that "Pressure against a wall rises with the square of the wind speed", that damage is increased when debris breaks the building envelope, which it says includes the roof, and that "Mobile homes and buildings in need of maintenance are most subject to wind damage."

You can read the plan on the parish Hazard Mitigation Plan Update page.

Mitigation Action 2

Titled "Adopt Building Codes and Ordinances Related to Hardening of Future Industrial, Commercial, and Residential Structures", with the Parish Council as lead agency and a medium priority. Its measures include "using materials rated for the appropriate windspeeds". Its status reads "New (Carried Over/Not Started from 2016 Update)".

Our reading, not the plan's: in the plan it is a proposal for future codes, and we have not found a parish ordinance that adopts it. What a commercial re-roof answers to is Chapter 15.

How the planning survey ranked it

The plan reports that survey respondents "had mixed ideas about the importance of both hardening of future industrial, commercial, and residential structures, and generator installation." In the plan's prioritized list of 13 activities, that hardening item is eleventh.

Our reading, not the plan's: with the plan treating wind rated materials as a proposal, the new roof assembly is addressed in the project scope against Chapter 15.

Does that apply to a re-roof or only to a new building

Why is the roof edge listed separately

Section 1504.6 gives metal edge systems on built-up, modified bitumen and single-ply roofs below a 2:12 slope their own wind design and test requirement, separate from the membrane. We name the edge system in the scope for that reason.
